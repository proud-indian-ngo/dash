# Governance: resolutions

> **Load when**: circular, circular resolution, resolution text, resolution template, issue, response, objection, conflict of interest on a circular, signature check, resolution number, register change on pass, withdraw, past resolution, DSC signing helper, `keyseal://`, certificate enrolment, Aadhaar eSign, Leegality, PAdES, long-term copies, re-timestamping.
> **Related**: `governance.md` (register, deed rules, vault, signing service)

## Lifecycle

| Step | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Draft | `governance.manage` | One resolution: the text (see [Text](#text)) and the template's register change |
| Issue | The office-holder the deed allows (version 1: the MT) | Picks up to ten drafts and their order into one circular, and whether it is signed with DSCs or Aadhaar eSign (see [Signing](#signing)). Refused when the board is too small, a register change doesn't fit the register as the drafts before it would leave it, or a threshold is out of reach. Freezes revision 1 and renders one PDF with every resolution and, per trustee in office, a signature field and an answers field (an eSign circular has neither; see [Aadhaar eSign](#aadhaar-esign)). The register is read again as the circular is saved; if the issuer's authority, the trustees in office or a threshold changed meanwhile, it is refused for a fresh issue |
| Respond | Each trustee the circular went to | Object to a resolution with a reason, or declare a conflict on it, which leaves its count. Then sign the circular once: the signature assents to every open resolution they haven't objected to or declared a conflict on. Ones decided before they sign are marked so, not assented to |
| Pass | | A resolution's signed assents reach its threshold |
| Fail | | Objections put a resolution's threshold out of reach, or the response date passes. Responses are taken through that day and refused after it |
| Withdraw | The issuer, while any resolution is open | With a reason; resolutions already decided stay as they are |

- Each resolution in a circular passes or fails on its own. After every response, withdrawal or close, the open ones are settled in order under the circular's lock. One whose register change only fits once an earlier open resolution passes (a designation after the appointment) waits for it, and fails with the reason if that one fails.

- Templates are code, not a table. They live in `packages/shared/src/resolutions.ts` with the tally, who may issue on a date, numbering and the signature-page layout, which the PDF and the signature fields both read so they line up.
- Passing takes the register lock, then the next number for the financial year under an advisory lock, and makes the register change in the same transaction. A cessation ends the trustee's latest open term begun before its date and the offices they still hold that day; a designation ends the office's holder on its start day. An office that already ended with its holder's term is left alone. A change the register can no longer take fails the resolution with the reason. Terms and offices keep the resolution that made them.
- A trustee sees the circulars sent to them without `governance.view`.
- Past paper resolutions are recorded with their numbers so the series continues. `governance.manage` can correct every recorded field under the register lock and the year's numbering lock; while a term, office, resolution or meeting cites it, the number, kind (which sets its tally and pass semantics) and date stay and it can't be deleted. Circulars signed on paper after go-live are not a supported method: a trustee signs with the DSC helper, in Acrobat or with Aadhaar eSign, and a paper one is only ever recorded as history.
- Resolutions decided at a board meeting share the numbering and register changes; see [meetings](governance-meetings.md).

## Text

A resolution's text is rich text, written in the rich text editor (`@pi-dash/editor/rich-text-editor`, see [editor](editor.md)) for circular drafts and agenda resolutions alike: paragraphs, headings 1 to 3, quotes, dividers, bold, italic, underline and strikethrough, web and email links, indents, numbered and bulleted lists, nested, and tables with a header row, column widths and cells merged across a row. Trustees sign the PDF, so the text offers only what the PDF prints faithfully: no images, colours, code or cells merged down a column.

- `resolution.body` holds the Plate JSON. Every write passes `resolutionBodySchema`, which parses it with `@pi-dash/shared/rich-text`. It refuses any other node or mark type, cells merged down a column, ragged rows, tables wider than eight columns, lists deeper than six levels, indents past four, and links other than http, https and mailto or over 2,000 characters. It allows 20,000 characters of plain text in 200,000 of JSON and stores the canonical form: types, children, marks, indents, link addresses, column spans and widths. Bodies stored before headings, quotes, links and merged cells parse to the same bytes, so none needed migrating.
- The draft form refuses a body without text (`resolutionBodyProblem`), and so do issue and notice (`richTextHasText`): empty lists and tables don't count.
- A template's text is rich text written with the builders in `@pi-dash/shared/rich-text`: the lead words "RESOLVED THAT" and "RESOLVED FURTHER THAT" are bold, and [placeholders] mark what to fill in; the budget and bank templates carry a table (the budget's Total spans Head and Purpose), the deed amendment a numbered list whose items quote the new clause. `templateBody` stores it. Switching template replaces the text only while it has no text or is still the old template's exactly, formatting included (`bodyUntouched`).
- Pages show the text read-only through the same preset. Anything that needs plain text uses `richTextToPlain`: blocks apart by a blank line, list items on lines with their marker, quoted lines after "> ", a divider as "---", a link as its text and address, table cells apart by " | ".
- A numbered list counts 1, then a, then i by the numbered lists it sits in, bulleted ones not counting (`orderedListStyle`). The editor, the pages, the PDF and plain text share that rule.
- Migration 0176 turned the plain-text bodies into paragraphs in SQL with the same rule as `plainTextToRichText`, ASCII whitespace only.
- The circular and the minutes draw it with `packages/pdf/src/rich-text.tsx` (see [PDF](pdf.md#rich-text)).

## Signatures

- Register numbers aren't printed: the signatures fix the bytes before the numbers exist. A short circular reference is printed instead.
- The signing service adds every empty field in one incremental update, so each signature appends to the same file. Each signature field locks its trustee's answers field (FieldMDP).
- Before a trustee signs, their answers ("Resolution 1: assents; Resolution 2: objects") are filled into their read-only answers field, and that copy is kept per latest PDF and set of answers. The fill is deterministic, so two requests for the same copy store the same bytes. The signature covers the answers, so the signed PDF records what each trustee assented and objected to. Changing an answer after starting means starting again.
- A signed copy must be that copy with exactly one more signature appended, intact, covering the whole file, and by that trustee by name (`matchSignerToTrustee`). It may not be a certification (DocMDP) signature, and may lock (FieldMDP) only that trustee's own fields, since either would make the later trustees' signatures fail. It must sit in the trustee's own box, which the copy already had; the helper and Acrobat sign there. Every earlier signature and every answers field must be unchanged, and no earlier signature may report a change beyond form filling, so a copy another trustee's signature has moved past is refused as stale. pyHanko judges only changes after a signature, so the validator also reviews everything appended to the copy the trustee was given: anything beyond form filling (signing a field) is refused, and so is any new widget, which pyHanko counts as form filling even for a new signature field, and any change to how an existing widget renders (its place, appearance, flags, look or read-only flag) other than the signed field's value and appearance.
- The new signature's certificate must chain to a root the signing service bundles (the CCA India roots), and so must a certificate at enrolment; a system root doesn't count. Every earlier signature must still pass that check, or the upload is refused and the trustee is asked to tell the Managing Trustee. pyHanko checks a certificate as of now, so one with a trusted timestamp that fails now is checked again as of the timestamp, with revocation data issued since and whatever the first check found: revoked after it was made, it still counts. Revocation is soft-fail: a certificate is refused only when known revoked, and issuer certificates, OCSP and CRL data are fetched only with `SIGNING_ALLOW_FETCHING=true` (off by default). Fetches and redirects use only public HTTP(S) destinations: DNS answers and the numeric socket destination are checked, including IPv4-mapped IPv6, so DNS rebinding cannot reach private addresses. The application lifespan owns and closes the fetch session. Indian CAs' OCSP servers (Verasys, eMudhra) drop connections from outside India, so from the German production host a fetch times out; pyHanko's soft-fail covers a refused connection but not a timeout, so the validator then checks again without fetching. A signature whose check still fails is reported with the exception's name, logged by the service, and the trustee is told it couldn't be checked rather than that the file changed.
- Issuing, and every other signed document (minutes, declarations, [utilisation certificates](finance-grants.md#utilisation-certificates)), needs the signing service: without it nothing is issued and signed uploads are refused, because they can't be checked. Every signed copy therefore has its fields, so the validator uses pyHanko's public form rule with no case for a copy without a form.
- The signed PDF a resolution passed on goes into the vault and is the evidence for the register entries it made. Terms and offices record the resolution that started them and the one that ended them (a cessation, a removal, or a designation replacing the holder) separately, so filing either one later, in any order, fills the right date's evidence; resolutions that pass on the same signed PDF share one document, filed once by its hash even when concurrent responses pass them. One passed by an objection or a declared conflict files the circular's latest signed PDF. Signed copies are stored by content, so a copy refused as stale never replaces an accepted one. The R2 orphan cleanup keeps every circular PDF, and an open circular's whole folder (signing copies, helper state) until it closes.

## Signing

A circular is signed one way, chosen at issue: with DSCs (the helper, or Acrobat as a fallback), or with Aadhaar eSign only. Every DSC copy goes through the checks above; eSign copies through their own, below. The internal signing API authenticates the bearer token before reading a body. JSON requests are capped while streaming at 64 MiB (including the signing state); PDFs retain the 25 MiB limit. Only `GET /health` is unauthenticated.

- **DSC helper** (KeySeal). Browsers can't reach PKCS#11 tokens, so the signature is made outside the browser and joined back on the server. `@keyseal/server` serves the job endpoint under `/api/signing` and the manifest at `/.well-known/keyseal.json`; job rows live in `private.signing_job` (token and claim nonce hashed), the PDF and the service's state in R2 under `governance/signing-jobs/` while the job is open.
  1. Enrolment, once per DSC: the trustee picks a certificate in the helper, and the dashboard keeps it if it is in the trustee's name, valid now, has an RSA, P-256 or P-384 key, chains to a CCA root and has the key usage pyHanko requires of a signer (non-repudiation), so the token's encryption certificate is refused. A renewed DSC is enrolled again.
  2. Signing needs a recent sign-in and an enrolled certificate. The page opens a `keyseal://` link with a one-time job token (15 minutes) and polls the job; if no helper opens it within five seconds, it points to the Signing helper page while the job stays open.
  3. The helper fetches the job, which allows only the enrolled certificate, posts it, and gets the attributes to sign once the service has reserved the trustee's field. It asks for the PIN in its own window (never stored or sent), signs on the token and posts the signature back. When it gives up (PIN cancelled, no token, wrong token), it posts the reason instead, which fails the job, so the page stops waiting and shows why.
  4. The service embeds the signature with a timestamp (PAdES B-T). The box shows the name large, then "Digitally signed by", the name and the date in India time, over a faint Proud Indian symbol (the plugin's `proud-indian` stamp). The signed copy then goes through the checks above; a refusal fails the job with its reason. The job endpoint has no session, so completions are audited in the trustee's name.
- **Acrobat**: download the copy with the trustee's answers filled in, sign the box with the token in Acrobat or Reader, upload the saved copy.

A job is completed one request at a time: the request finishing it holds a five-minute lease, and `@keyseal/server` refuses a second meanwhile. One that takes over a lapsed lease and finds the trustee's signature already recorded since the job began settles it as signed.

Issuing notifies each trustee once per circular. The resolutions one response, withdrawal or close decides go out as one notice to the trustees and secretaries, with each outcome, through the `notify-resolution-decided` job, so a failed delivery is retried. The closing job queues its notice in the transaction that decides the circular, so a failed enqueue leaves the circular open for the next run.

Trustees download the KeySeal helper from the Signing helper page. It asks once per site whether to trust it and shows the site with every signature. The job endpoint refuses a helper older than `MIN_HELPER_VERSION` in `signing-jobs.ts` and fails the job with "Update the signing helper".

### Aadhaar eSign

Offered when `LEEGALITY_API_URL` and `LEEGALITY_AUTH_TOKEN` are set, for circulars, minutes, declarations and utilisation certificates, chosen when each is made; the DSC copies, uploads and helper refuse an eSign document. On a circular, a signature assents to every resolution the trustee hasn't objected to or declared a conflict on in the dashboard; the PDF carries no answers.

- **Issue.** The PDF is rendered with the boxes drawn and no fields, plus an empty form (pyHanko can't review a signature that adds a document's first form), and sent to Leegality once with every trustee in office invited at once to sign in their own box. Leegality sends no invitations itself. Each trustee needs an email (their record's, else their account's), and no two may share one; a response that leaves out an invitee is refused. Leegality's copy must show the same pages and catalog as ours, and becomes the latest PDF on record. Invitations end with the response date, so it can be at most 30 days away. Any failure deletes the Leegality document.
- **Sign.** The trustee's link is fetched from Leegality when they open it (never stored), after a recent sign-in.
- **Record.** Their eSign is taken when they press "I've signed", when the issuer or `governance.manage` checks, or by the scheduled job ([jobs](jobs.md#schedules)). Leegality's copy must extend the latest on record and only add signed fields. Each new signature must be intact, cover its revision, chain to a CCA root, lock and certify nothing, carry a timestamp (the certificate lives about 30 minutes, so the timestamp keeps it trusted), sit in an unsigned trustee's box, and be in that one trustee's name (the Aadhaar name counts). Each is recorded in signing order on the copy as it left it, as of its timestamp's date, so one made by the response date counts even when taken after it; one made later isn't taken. Each is audited as `circular.sign` in the signer's name.
- **Answers.** Once a trustee has signed on Leegality, their eSign is taken and objections or conflicts from them are refused. A trustee left with nothing to assent to has their invitation revoked.
- **Close.** Once the circular is decided or withdrawn, Leegality's audit trail (when every trustee signed) goes into the vault and the document is deleted, revoking the other invitations; a failure is retried by the job. The close job leaves due eSign circulars to the eSign job, which takes their eSigns first.
- **Other documents.** Minutes (the chair), a declaration (its trustee) and a utilisation certificate (the Head of the Organisation, then the Chief Finance Officer, in that order) are sent the same way when made, for 30 days; each signer needs a linked account. Making one again first withdraws the earlier Leegality document, or fails. eSigns are taken by the signer's "I've signed", a check by `governance.manage` (minutes) or `finance.manage` (certificates), or the job, and accepted as a signed upload is, against the copy checked, as of when signed. Once accepted, the audit trail is filed; one made again, dropped, discarded, cancelled or lapsed is withdrawn without a trail, a failed delete retried by the job.
- **Checks in the plugin.** Leegality gives each new field the object number of the previous revision's cross-reference stream, which KeySeal's check reads as a change; the plugin's check frees only that number, and only when nothing refers to it. Protean eGov CA 2022, which the timestamps' chain needs, ships with the plugin. pyHanko's strict reader refuses these files, so their vault checks and long-term copies go through the plugin, chosen by whether the document was signed with eSign; no revocation data is fetched for eSign certificates.

## Long-term copies

A passed circular's signed PDF gets a long-term copy at once (revocation data and a document timestamp, PAdES B-LTA), and downloads serve that copy; a [utilisation certificate](finance-grants.md#utilisation-certificates) gets one once both trustees have signed. A monthly job adds a new document timestamp a year after the last, and makes any copy whose making failed in full (revocation data included); a failed one stays due.

Not built: certifying (DocMDP) first signatures; the first signature is an approval signature. Not yet tried with real Acrobat files: that they carry the answers field's lock into the signature, and that they don't rewrite an existing field's appearance in place, which the validator now reads as a change to earlier signatures.
