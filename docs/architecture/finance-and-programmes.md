# Finance and Programmes (design)

> **Status**: design agreed; delivery in progress. Shipped: Phase 1 dimension tables, reference data, `finance.view`/`finance.manage`, and the Programmes settings page. Also shipped: `programme_id` on teams and events with the production backfill (migration 0114), a Programme column and filter on the reimbursement, vendor payment and team events tables, and a Programme column in the financial-year export plus a spending-by-programme chart, all derived through "Charged to" (see "Charged to" below; advance payments are charged to nothing, so they show "—" and match no Programme filter value; a programme frozen on the posted document waits for expense posting), a Projects settings page, the fiscal-year job, and the party foundation: `party` and `party_role` synced through Zero, `party_private` and `party_bank_account` in a server-only schema barrel with AES-GCM encrypted identifiers (`DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY`), `user.party_id`, the `donors.view`, `donors.manage` and `finance.view_sensitive` permissions, and a Donors directory. Decision: contact details are readable by `donors.view` (admins and finance), the full identifier only by `finance.view_sensitive`, and the reveal is audited although it is a read. The private tables live in the Postgres schema `private`, outside zero-cache's publication, so they are never replicated. Also shipped: the vendor migration onto parties (migration 0116 and a one-off backfill, run in production in October 2026; the emptied legacy table is dropped): every vendor is a party with the `vendor` role under its old UUID, the existing `vendor` row acts as this design's `vendor_profile` (name mirror, GSTIN, approval status, synced through Zero), and contact, PAN and bank details live in `party_private` and `party_bank_account`, read and written only through audited server functions (`apps/web/src/functions/vendors.ts`); replaced bank accounts stay as inactive history. Also shipped: the bigint paise migration (migration 0118): reimbursement, advance payment and vendor payment line items and vendor payment transactions store `amount_paise bigint`, backfilled exactly from the old `numeric(12,2)` rupees with a guard that aborts on more than two decimals, and every conversion goes through `@pi-dash/shared/money` (exact string parsing, safe-integer checks, the existing INR display and export formats). Also shipped (Phase 2, first layer): `contribution` and `other_income` (migration 0119) as manual staging records on an Income page (`/income`, readable with `donors.view`, `finance.view`, `donors.manage` or `finance.manage`, since managing implies seeing): contributions are written through Zero mutators with `donors.manage`, other income with `finance.manage`, both through the central mutation audit boundary (ids and field names only). Each contribution names a party with an active donor, sponsor, grantor or intermediary role (CSR money comes from sponsors, sometimes through an intermediary such as Goodera, and grants from grantors; the field stays labelled "Donor" and the picker shows each party's roles), its channel, the Form 113 reporting mode and donation type ("other" needs a description), the active org account it was deposited into (none for in-kind gifts, which need a description and valuation basis), a fund (default "General Fund (Domestic)") and an optional programme and project, where the project fixes the programme. The channel decides the account and the reporting mode (`packages/shared/src/contribution-channel.ts`, enforced by the form and the contribution mutators): Razorpay goes into the Razorpay clearing account chosen in Settings (`app_config` `razorpay_clearing_account_id`) and give.do into a gateway clearing account other than Razorpay's (the form fixes the one named for give.do), both reported electronic and shown locked; UPI, NEFT/IMPS and cheque go into a bank account, cash into a bank or cash account, and the form fills in the only one when there is one; every channel fixes its mode (electronic, cash or in kind) except cheque, which may be reported as other for a bearer cheque. Changing the channel resets the mode to its default and an account that no longer fits; an edited record still shows values that break the rules, but saving needs a fitting combination, except that a refund or processed settlement keeps the account it fixed. Imports follow the same rules: an upload, a give.do payout and a mapping review refuse an account that cannot take the batch's channel (the upload and payout pickers offer only fitting accounts and fix give.do's), and every staged row whose channel does not fit the batch's account is a row error at review that a run never imports (`withChannelRules` in `apps/web/src/lib/income-import/normalize.ts`). A Razorpay import batch is judged by its own account (a gateway clearing account, the setting when the batch opened), so queued payments still import after the setting changes. Changing the Razorpay clearing account after go-live is a migration: earlier unsettled Razorpay gifts stay in the old account and must be moved before they can be edited (a refund or settlement keeps the account it fixed), and the Settings field warns about it. While no Razorpay clearing account is set, the give.do rule accepts any gateway clearing account. The donation type decides the programme (`packages/shared/src/contribution-programme.ts`, enforced by the form, the contribution mutators on save, and imports as a row error against the batch's programme): a specific grant or CSR gift needs the programme it's for (a project sets it), a corpus gift has none (the form hides the field and clears programme and project), and voluntary or other gifts may name one; existing records are held to it only when saved. The donation type also decides the fund (`donationTypeFundIssue`, same file, enforced in the same places, imports as a row error against the batch's fund): a corpus gift goes to the Corpus Fund, which the form selects and locks when the corpus type is chosen, and only a corpus gift goes there (the form leaves the Corpus Fund out of the list for other types). Gifts recorded before this rule against the wrong fund are not moved: the contributions table flags them ("Check fund" beside the donation type) and the detail sheet says what the books do with them; editing one fixes it (the form moves a corpus gift to the Corpus Fund on save). Manual entries are created as `received`, source `manual`, deduction eligibility `unknown`, and can be edited or deleted only while `pending` or `received`. The page shows this financial year's received total, count and donors, and a "Missing donor details" count (donors with money received this year but no ID number or address), computed by a server function under the same four permissions that returns party IDs only; its filter shows only this year's received gifts from those donors; the donor sheet gains a Giving section. "Received" means status `received` or `receipted` (`CONTRIBUTION_RECEIVED_STATUSES` in `@pi-dash/shared/income`, used by the stats, the Giving section and the server query alike): pending money has not arrived, and refunded or reversed money has gone back. The Unallocated programme holder is refused on contributions. Edit forms keep a retired account, fund, programme, project or giver in its picker, marked "(inactive)". An Organisation accounts settings page (`/settings/org-accounts`, `finance.view` to see, `finance.manage` to add, edit or deactivate) manages `org_account` name, kind, fund source and FCRA role; the FCRA role is stored as `none` unless the fund source is FCRA, accounts are deactivated rather than deleted, and bank details wait for an encrypted server-only layer, so `org_account` keeps syncing through Zero with no sensitive column. Also shipped (Phase 2, layer 3b-1, migration 0120): CSV import of contributions from generic spreadsheets at `/income/import` (`donors.manage`), a four-step page (Upload, Map columns, Review, Import). `import_batch` (public, synced to the four income permissions for the Imports tab: settings and counts only, including the file name) records each upload; `private.import_row` holds each row as uploaded in `raw` (the file itself is not stored, only its SHA-256), `private.external_identity` records (provider, provider account = the deposit org account, entity type `payment`, external ID) uniquely with the contribution it became and a payload hash (row hashes and fingerprints are HMAC-SHA256 under a key derived from `DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY` with HKDF, prefixed with `h1:`, which names the hashing scheme, not the key: changing the key changes every hash, see "Import hashes and the key" below; the batch default channel is left out of the payload hash, a channel stated in the file is not), and `contribution.import_batch_id` lets the Income table filter by import. Uploads are parsed with papaparse on both sides, and rows with broken quoting or more cells than the header are errors; amounts accept Indian and international grouping and are capped at `MAX_AMOUNT_PAISE`; numeric dates follow the date format chosen at mapping (year-first and month-name dates are unambiguous); the mapping is remembered per source. Rows are classified server-side on every review and again inside the import transaction: an identity seen before (in the database or earlier in the same file) with the same payload links (`linked_existing`), with a different payload is a conflict and is never overwritten; a file without an external ID column falls back to a fingerprint of date, amount, donor name and reference, with a warning that two identical gifts without a reference are then treated as one. The donor is picked automatically only on an exact stored identifier match: candidates are narrowed by ID type and the clear last four characters and decrypted server-side, with no blind index; two parties sharing an identifier, or matches by email or phone, are proposals the reviewer must accept, and rows left undecided are skipped. Rows in one file sharing an identifier follow the first such row, so they create or pick one donor; when that row needs a choice, the later rows take the donor chosen there; same email or phone within a file is proposed. A reference match, or the same donor, day and amount, flags a possible duplicate that must be confirmed. New donors go through `savePartyInTransaction` (the `saveDonor` path) inside the run, and every row is checked against the donor form's rules at review, so a row shown as importable cannot fail when its donor is saved; invalid PANs, emails or phones are dropped with a warning, so the donor lands in "Missing donor details". Every donor created or matched gets one `private.collection_event` per batch (unique on party and batch; basis `voluntary_provision_7a`, purpose "donation receipt and statutory filing", channel `generic_csv`, note "collected directly by the organisation; notice version not recorded"). A run is one transaction under an advisory lock, which the retention job also takes, so it imports every decided row or none; retrying from the batch page reprocesses only rows not yet imported or linked. Upload, review and run are audited (`import_batch.create`, `.review`, `.run`) with counts only; a file that is not UTF-8 (a NUL character or a UTF-16 byte order mark) is refused at parsing, and any other failure in upload, review, run or the batch page is logged with the Postgres error code, constraint, table, batch ID and step only, then replaced by a generic error with no cause, because a query error's message carries its parameters, here row cells; error messages never quote a cell; the batch rows are read through a server function that needs `donors.view` or `donors.manage`; identifiers in it are always masked to their last four characters (also PAN-shaped text, with or without spaces or hyphens, and Aadhaar-shaped runs of twelve digits, in any other cell or message; a 12-digit UPI reference is therefore also shown as its last four), and the column samples used to continue an import go to `donors.manage` only while rows remain to import. Retention: `import_row` carries `retention_policy_version`, `retain_until`, `retention_basis` and `legal_hold`; the daily `expire-import-rows` job nulls `raw` 180 days after a batch's last run, and deletes the rows of a batch never imported 30 days after upload, marking it `expired`; rows of a batch in `importing` are never nulled or deleted, and a batch left `importing` for more than 24 hours (a run that died) is reset to `failed` by the job first, so it can be retried or expires like any failed batch; held rows are skipped, and contributions, identities and collection events are never touched. `collection_event` has no retention columns yet: it is kept for the life of the donor record, since it evidences the basis for holding that data, until the DPDP layer adds retention. Also shipped (layer 3b-2, no migration): the give.do (GiveIndia) source. Its 19-column export is mapped by an exact-header preset (`GIVE_DO_COLUMNS` in `apps/web/src/lib/income-import/fields.ts`; Time, Donor Complete Address, Subscription Type, Parent Donation and tags fill nothing but stay in `raw`), and the mapping step flags missing or extra columns while staying editable. The source fixes day-first dates, channel `give_do` (so reporting mode `electronic_or_account_payee`) and PAN as the type of the Donor PAN column; the upload defaults to the active `gateway_clearing` account named like give.do or GiveIndia, or asks for a pick with a link to Organisation accounts. A Currency field (also offered to generic files) refuses any row not in INR with "Foreign currency: FCRA is not activated", so foreign money is never imported as domestic. Transaction ID is the external identity under provider `give_do`. Every give.do run ensures one well-known party, "GiveIndia (give.do)" (`GIVE_INDIA_PARTY_ID`, organisation, active `intermediary` role; also in `scripts/seed.ts`), and sets it as `intermediary_party_id` on each contribution, shown as "Through" on the contribution sheet; platform intermediaries (`PLATFORM_INTERMEDIARY_PARTY_IDS`, `canGive` in `@pi-dash/shared/income`) are never offered or accepted as the giver, nor matched as a donor, unless they also hold a donor, sponsor or grantor role. Collection events for give.do donors use channel `give_do_csv` and the note "donor gave details to give.do (GiveIndia) and saw GiveIndia's notice, not ours". Accepting an existing donor proposed by email or phone offers "Fill missing details" (on by default; the review names the fields): the row's ID number, address (all lines together, only when the donor has no address line at all), email or phone are added only where the donor has none, through `fillMissingPartyDetails`, which locks the party, re-reads it, and saves through `savePartyInTransaction` with the versions it read, so identifiers are encrypted as in the donor form and nothing stored is replaced; the run's audit metadata carries the count of such decisions only. Recurring give.do donations (Subscription Type, Parent Donation) get no special handling yet; both stay in `raw`. Also shipped (layer 3c-1, migration 0123): Razorpay Payment Page ingestion, on only when `RAZORPAY_KEY_ID` and `RAZORPAY_WEBHOOK_SECRET` are both set; the key id's prefix is the mode (`live` or `test`), which is the external identity's provider account, so live and test payment IDs never mix. `POST /api/razorpay/webhook` verifies `X-Razorpay-Signature` (hex HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body, constant-time; 401 on a bad or missing signature, 400 on bad JSON, 404 when off; a body over 1 MB is refused with 413, by Content-Length or while streaming, a body stalled for 3 seconds with 408, and one the client aborted mid-read with 400), records the time in app_config `razorpay_last_webhook_at`, and for `payment.captured` and `order.paid` with a captured payment stages it as a row of its India day's `razorpay_api` batch (one per mode and day, keyed in `file_hash`, named "Razorpay payments, 12 Oct 2026"), then enqueues `razorpay-sync-payment` keyed by the payment ID; a signed payment event whose payment cannot be read is fetched by ID when `RAZORPAY_KEY_SECRET` is set, and otherwise acknowledged and logged; other events, including refunds and `payment.failed`, are acknowledged and logged. With a `rzp_test_` key on a production server (`NODE_ENV=production`) ingestion is refused: deliveries are verified, acknowledged with 200 and logged as "test mode refused" with the event and payment ID; no payment data is stored and no job is queued (only the last-webhook time is written, as for every verified delivery), and Settings → Integrations shows a blocking error. Staging stores allow-listed payment fields (no card, bank or VPA details) and, of the notes, only those whose key matches a configured or default notes key (ignoring case and punctuation), at most 20, each cut to 500 characters, all flattened to dot keys. Decision (data minimisation over re-reading): a notes key added to the settings later cannot be read from payments staged before it. A staged row is pending with reason `queued`; a later copy only refreshes a row still pending, or reopens one that failed as `not_found` or `not_captured`. A payment is found by the ID in its values or, once those are nulled, by the external identity it was imported or linked under, so a redelivery never stages it twice. The job records a payment the API does not know as an error with reason `not_found`, and one no longer captured (refunded before the job ran, say) as an error with reason `not_captured`, logging Razorpay's status word only; when its last retry fails, the row waits with reason `sync_failed`. The job fetches the payment from the API when `RAZORPAY_KEY_SECRET` is set (and stages that copy), otherwise uses the staged webhook copy, then classifies the row with the CSV import's rules inside the import transaction: amount, fee and tax are paise, the fee kept is Razorpay's fee minus its tax (the fee includes GST), `received_on` is the India date of `created_at` (Razorpay sends no capture time), channel `razorpay`, reporting mode electronic, type voluntary, source import, the configured clearing account, General Fund (Domestic), reference and external ID the payment ID. Donor details come from the notes, read through an editable key list (app_config `razorpay_notes_keys`, matched ignoring case and punctuation); a payment without a name is named after its email or phone with a warning. A row that needs no decision (an exact PAN match, or a new donor) is imported at once as the system (`created_by`, `decided_by` and the party's `created_by` null; a system save never counts as a create retry); a PAN-less email or phone match, a hand-entered gift with the same reference or donor, day and amount, waits as `needs_review`; a changed payload under a known payment ID is a conflict; non-INR payments ("Foreign currency: FCRA is not activated", through the same `isRupeeCurrency` guard as the CSV Currency field) and uncaptured payments are errors. The identity's payload hash is taken from the payment itself (amount, currency, time, contact, fee, tax and the notes kept at staging), not the normalised row, so a later refund never turns a redelivery into a conflict. Because the notes are minimised before hashing, the hash depends on the notes keys; that is safe because a decided payment is never staged again (a redelivery is found by its ID or external identity and leaves the row alone), so a copy flattened under changed keys is never compared with the stored hash. Without a clearing account every payment waits (`no_clearing_account`) in a batch with no account; when the batch's account has since been deactivated, its payments wait with reason `clearing_account_inactive` and Settings → Integrations says so. Saving an account in Settings attaches it, under the staging lock, to batches with no usable account and re-enqueues the waiting payments' sync jobs keyed by payment ID; the account is read inside the staging transaction, so a batch is never opened without an account saved meanwhile. The notes keys can be saved without choosing an account. Reviewers decide waiting payments on the batch page ("Review N payments" opens the importer at its Review step). `import_batch.pending_count` counts every pending row (queued for sync, sync failed, needs review, no or inactive clearing account) and rows a run left undecided, for every source, and `skipped_count` no longer includes them; a Razorpay payment a reviewer leaves undecided stays pending with `needs_review`; the Imports tab shows "Waiting: N" and a Waiting column per batch, Settings → Integrations shows the total as "Waiting", and the batch page breaks the waiting rows down by reason (needs review, syncing, sync failed, no clearing account, account inactive), so a payment syncing for a few seconds is not mistaken for one a reviewer must decide. Each donor gets one collection event per batch with channel `razorpay_payment_page`, note "donor submitted details on our Razorpay Payment Page". Razorpay batches are created `completed`; their rows are staged with no `retain_until` (`retention_basis` `import_payload_awaiting_outcome`) and get the imported rows' 180 days once they reach an outcome (imported, linked, skipped or error), so a payment waiting for review or an account keeps its values; the retention job never nulls `raw` of a pending row in any batch, and never deletes Razorpay rows unreviewed. Webhooks and jobs are outside the audit ledger; logs carry event, event ID, payment ID and outcome, and errors go through the import's safe-error helper (`apps/web/src/lib/safe-errors.ts`): only the error name, Postgres code, constraint and table are logged or stored by pg-boss, and the settings functions return a generic message. A failed or dead review run leaves a Razorpay batch `completed`, never `failed`. Payment payload and row hashes use the import's keyed `h1:` scheme (see "Import hashes and the key"), and a Razorpay batch never sends its cells as column samples. Settings → Integrations (`finance.view`; saving the clearing account and notes keys needs `finance.manage` and is audited as `razorpay_settings.update` with field names) shows status and mode, API or webhook source, the webhook URL and events, the last webhook time and the waiting count, warns about payments waiting on a deactivated account or after a failed sync, and shows a blocking error when a test key runs in production. app_config syncs through Zero to every internal user; the three Razorpay keys hold an account ID, note key names and a timestamp, never secrets or personal data. Also shipped (layer 3c-2, migration 0125): Razorpay reconcile, backfill and refunds. A captured payment is now any payment with `captured: true`, so one refunded since (status `refunded`) is recorded like any other and its refunds are applied separately; a payment never captured (`captured: false`, including one Razorpay auto-refunded without capture) is still an error `not_captured`, and rows already in error `not_captured` that were captured are reopened by the next sync or reconcile (this replaces 3c-1's "no longer captured" rule). One job, `razorpay-reconcile {from, to, reason: scheduled | manual | backfill}`, runs on a pg-boss `exclusive` queue (one queued or running run at a time, never retried, a 60-second heartbeat so a run whose process died fails within a couple of minutes, and stopped by itself before its 4-hour expiry, so two runs never overlap); it refuses a test key on a production server, as the webhook does. It first syncs payments stuck pending with reason `queued` or `sync_failed` for over an hour (a sync job that expired, or a fetch-by-ID job with no day), once per run, within the run's time budget and writing its progress; with the key secret through the run's paced API client, without it from the staged webhook copy. Then, with `RAZORPAY_KEY_SECRET` only, it walks India days one at a time (each window from midnight IST to the next midnight; Razorpay's `to` is inclusive, so a payment at exactly midnight is listed twice, which staging absorbs) and, for each, lists payments (`GET /v1/payments?from&to&count=100&skip`, paging until a page is short) and refunds (`GET /v1/refunds`) through a paced client (calls one at a time at least 250 ms apart; a 429 waits for Retry-After or 1, 2, 4… seconds, up to five times), and puts every captured payment through the webhook's own path (same staging, identity and rules), so a webhook and a reconcile never count a payment twice. Afterwards it recounts `pending_count` for every batch it touched; every recount (the reconcile's, the sync job's and a review run's) first locks the batch row (`select … for update`), and staging adds its one under the same row lock, so a recount racing a staging can no longer leave the count one short. A staged payment keeps its ID in `private.import_row.external_id`, which retention keeps when it nulls `raw` (rows staged before the column existed get it at that moment), so a skipped or failed payment is found again and never staged twice by a redelivery or a backfill. The schedule runs every 6 hours over today and the three days before, registered whenever ingestion is on (key id and webhook secret) and the key is not a test key in production; without the key secret it only syncs stuck payments. Decision: the planned backfill CLI is replaced by "Import past payments" in Settings → Integrations, so the founder never needs a shell against production: a From date (default the start of the financial year) and a To date (default India's today), real dates only, at most 400 days, key secret only, queued as `reason: backfill`; payments land in their India-day batches as before. "Sync now" queues the last three days (`reason: manual`); without the key secret it still runs and says it only retries stuck payments. Both need `finance.manage`, are audited as `razorpay.sync` (no metadata) and `razorpay.backfill` (from, to and day count), and are refused while pg-boss holds an open run, which is also what the page shows as running, so the button never offers a run the queue would refuse; a refused request puts back the run state it replaced only if no other request has written since. The run's progress (days done of total) and the last result (start and finish, reason, window, whether it listed from the API, counts of payments checked, new, already known, imported, waiting, refunds applied, waiting and changed, stuck payments retried and errors, ok or failed with a safe code such as `api_502` or `time_limit`) live in app_config `razorpay_reconcile_run` and `razorpay_last_reconcile`, dates and counts only; a payment or refund that fails inside a run is logged with its IDs and the safe error fields. Settings → Integrations shows "Last sync: 14 Oct, 10:05 · 12 payments checked · 1 new · 0 errors" (or "Never"), "Syncing… 3 of 400 days" while a run is active, and polls until it finishes. Refunds live in `contribution_refund` (public, synced through Zero under the four income permissions, read-only to clients and written only by the server): contribution (restrict), amount in paise (> 0), refunded on (India date of the refund), source (`razorpay`, with `manual` reserved), reference (`rfnd_…`, unique per source), and two flags, `refund_after_receipt` and `amount_changed`, kept separately so one never clears the other. Only `processed` refunds count. A signed `refund.processed` webhook holds the refund's verified copy (IDs, amount, currency, time) in `private.pending_refund` and queues `razorpay-sync-refund` keyed by the refund ID; with the key secret the job fetches the refund and retries while the API does not yet show it processed, otherwise it uses the copy; `refund.created`, `refund.failed` and `refund.speed_changed` are acknowledged and logged. Each refund is an external identity (provider `razorpay`, provider account the mode, entity type `refund`, the refund ID) whose target is the refunded contribution for now (the posting layer will point it at a refund movement), so applying it again is a no-op and a copy with another amount is flagged `amount_changed` once, never written over. Applying one runs under the import run lock, locks the contribution, records the refund, and when its refunds reach the amount turns a `received` or `pending` contribution `refunded`; a partial refund leaves the status and shows "Partly refunded ₹X" (derived, never stored); a `receipted` contribution keeps its status and the refund is flagged `refund_after_receipt`; a `reversed` one only gets the record. No posting and no movement is made. Decision (refund before payment, revised after review): a refund whose payment is not a contribution yet stays in `private.pending_refund` with no time limit, and the import transaction that creates or links that payment's contribution (the sync job, the reconcile, or a reviewer's run) applies it there and then; because both take the import run lock, a refund held while its payment is being imported is never missed by both. The reconcile's refund listing holds and applies refunds of its window the same way, and with the key secret a payment synced, reconciled or imported by a reviewer that Razorpay reports as refunded (`amount_refunded`) has its refunds fetched (`GET /v1/payments/:id/refunds`) and applied, which also catches refunds missed outside the four-day window when a backfill covers the payment's day. Settings → Integrations shows "N refunds waiting for their payment" when any are held. A recorded refund fixes what the provider reported: the amount, donor, date, account and channel can no longer be edited and the contribution can no longer be deleted, while programme, project, fund, notes and the other classification fields stay editable; the Zero mutator locks the contribution row before checking, as the refund apply does, and the form shows those fields as fixed. "Received" totals subtract partial refunds: `netReceivedPaise` in `@pi-dash/shared/income` (amount less refunds when the status counts as received, never below zero) is used by the Income stats, the donor sheet's Giving section and the "Missing donor details" server query alike, and `CONTRIBUTION_RECEIVED_STATUSES` keeps its meaning. The Income table shows "Partly refunded ₹X" beside the status, the contribution sheet lists its refunds (date, amount, reference, flags), and Settings → Integrations shows "Needs attention" when a refund hit a receipted contribution or Razorpay reported a refund again with another amount (there is no way yet to clear the flags). The contribution sheet of a Razorpay import (channel `razorpay`, source `import`, reference a payment ID) has "Open in Razorpay", a link to `https://dashboard.razorpay.com/app/payments/<pay_id>` in a new tab; the reference already holds the payment ID, so nothing private reaches the browser, and the dashboard opens in whichever mode the person has selected there. The payments and refunds listings are account-wide: every captured payment on the Razorpay account is treated as a donation, which holds because the account takes only donations (founder, 14 October 2026); a filter would be needed if it ever takes other payments. Also shipped (layer 3c-3, Razorpay settlements): every payout Razorpay makes from the clearing balance into the bank is recorded with exactly the payments, refunds, transfers and adjustments it covered. `gateway_settlement` (public, synced through Zero to `finance.view` and `finance.manage` only, not `donors.view`; read-only to clients, written only by the server) holds the provider (`razorpay`), provider account (the mode), the settlement ID (`setl_…`, unique per provider and mode), the clearing account configured when it was first seen (nullable), status (`created`, `processed`, `failed`), settled on (India date of the settlement's `created_at`), the net amount paid, totals recounted from its lines on every write (gross of its payment lines, refunds, fees net of GST, GST, payment and line counts, so the table never syncs every line), the payout's own fee net of GST and its GST (an instant settlement; null when Razorpay reports none), the count of recon lines naming it that could not be read (another currency or an unknown shape; the largest count seen is kept), the UTR and an `amount_changed` flag; `gateway_settlement_item` holds each line: type (`payment`, `refund`, `transfer`, `adjustment`), the Razorpay ID, credit, debit, fee net of GST and GST (as contributions store them; Razorpay's own `fee` includes GST), settled at, and nullable links to the contribution (through the payment's external identity) and to the `contribution_refund` (through its reference), unique per settlement, type and ID. Razorpay's `notes` and `description` on recon lines are never read or stored. Decision: no `external_identity` row for settlements; the unique (provider, provider account, settlement ID) on `gateway_settlement` is the identity, because `external_identity` requires a contribution target today, and a settlement targets the account movement the posting layer will create. Settlements come from the API only (`GET /v1/settlements?from&to&count&skip` and `GET /v1/settlements/recon/combined?year&month&day&count=1000&skip`, field names checked against Razorpay's API reference; an instant settlement's ID `setlod_…` is accepted and fetched from `GET /v1/settlements/ondemand/:id`, its `amount_settled` read as the amount and `reversed` as failed; one still created, initiated or partly processed is not recorded until it is final, so a partial amount never becomes the first amount), so they need `RAZORPAY_KEY_SECRET`; without it Settings → Integrations says so and `settlement.processed` webhooks are acknowledged only. The reconcile run, after each India day's payments and refunds, lists that day's settlements and recon lines, groups the lines by settlement, fetches by ID any settlement the recon names but the listing missed, and records each one; Sync now and Import past payments therefore cover settlements in their window too, and the last run counts settlements found and new and lines linked and still unlinked, each settlement once even when the run meets it on two days. A signed `settlement.processed` webhook queues `razorpay-sync-settlement {settlementId}` (stately, keyed by settlement ID, retried with backoff), which fetches the settlement and the recon of its India day, then of the next day (once it has begun) and the day before when its own day lists none of its lines; a processed settlement still without lines throws, so the job retries with backoff instead of recording an empty payout. A test key on a production server records nothing, as elsewhere. Recording runs under the import run lock: it upserts the settlement (status and UTR updated; an amount reported differently later sets `amount_changed` and keeps the first), upserts lines by their unique key, links unlinked lines, and recounts the totals. A line whose payment is not a contribution yet (not imported, waiting for review, or ignored) stays unlinked and is linked by the import transaction that creates or links that payment's contribution, as held refunds are; a refund line is linked when the refund is recorded. Settlements never create or change a contribution and make no posting or movement (the posting layer will make each a transfer out of clearing and into the bank); a payment line's fee or GST that differs from the stored fee of a contribution the Razorpay import created is flagged on the settlement sheet, never written over (a contribution recorded by hand and linked later holds no fee, so it is not flagged). Decision (founder, 14 October 2026): once a processed settlement has paid a contribution out, it can no longer be deleted and its amount, donor, date, account and channel are fixed, as after a refund; the Zero mutator checks this under the contribution's row lock (linking a line takes a key-share lock on that row, so the check holds), the form shows the fields as fixed and the sheet hides Delete (finance holders read the synced settlement; others ask a server function that returns only whether the contribution is locked; while unknown both treat it as locked), and the line's foreign key stays `set null`. Programme, project, fund, notes and the other classification fields stay editable. "Awaiting settlement" (what Razorpay still owes, for the server's mode) is worked out line by line, not as a running balance, so payments never imported, deleted contributions and adjustments cannot pull it off: the net (amount less Razorpay's fee and GST) of every contribution with a Razorpay payment identity of that mode whose status is received, receipted or refunded and whose payment has no line in a processed settlement, less every refund of those contributions with no line in a processed settlement. Refunded gifts count because their refund is subtracted; leaving the gift out would take the refund off twice. Only Razorpay refunds of the counted gifts are subtracted. A gift recorded by hand and linked to a Razorpay payment holds no fee, so it counts gross until settled. ## Charged to

Every reimbursement and vendor payment says which work it served, in `charged_to` (enum `expense_charged_to`): **event** (the event's programme; events carry no project), **programme** (`charged_programme_id`, with an optional `charged_project_id` under it, for ongoing costs with no event, such as the rent of a teaching space), or **organisation** (admin: audit fee, bank charges, website, trust paperwork). It is independent of the expense category, which records what was bought. One target per request; different purposes go on separate requests. Advance payments are discontinued and carry no charge.

- **Rule**: `expenseProgrammeOf(request)` in `packages/shared/src/expense-charge.ts` returns `{ programmeId, projectId, isAdmin }`, and every place that needs a request's programme goes through it: the request tables and Programme filter (`apps/web/src/lib/request-programme.ts`, "Admin" for organisation), the spending-by-programme chart, the financial-year export's Programme, Project and Charged to columns, and expense posting when it comes. Request headers show "Programme · Name", or "Admin".
- **Database**: checks on both tables require an event for `event` (and no charged programme or project), a programme and no event for `programme`, and none of them for `organisation`. A composite foreign key `(charged_project_id, charged_programme_id)` → `project(id, programme_id)` refuses a project under another programme, and a project with expenses charged to it can't move to another programme. Deleting a team whose events carry charged expenses is refused, since the event link must stay; a later improvement may instead convert those expenses to the event's programme.
- **Who picks**: the claimant on the form (a segmented control defaulting to Event), changeable by editing while the request is pending. Finance (`finance.manage`, or `requests.approve` with `requests.view_all`, as for approval) changes it from the request's Charged to card at approval or later through `reimbursement.setChargedTo` / `vendorPayment.setChargedTo`, never on a rejected request or on their own. The history entry reads "Charged to: Event · X → Programme · Y"; the audit ledger records the field names only. Once a request is posted to the books (layer 3-3a), `setChargedTo` refuses the change ("This request is in the books, so what it is charged to can't be changed"; founder decision): the server checks for a standing posting, and the card hides Change and shows "In the books" to book readers. Before that it is open at any other status. The approval dialog shows what the request is charged to.
- **Pickers**: the programme picker never offers the Unallocated holder for a new pick; a request already on it keeps it until changed. Projects sync only with `finance.view`, so claimants pick the programme and finance adds the project.
- **Backfill** (migrations 0137 to 0141): requests with an event became `event`; requests without one became `programme` on Unallocated (0138), and 0140 then applies the Phase 0 mapping: a request whose submitter is in exactly one team, with a real programme, moves to that team's programme. Finance clears the rest with the "Unallocated · N" button on the reimbursements and vendor payments lists, which applies the "To clear" filter (charged straight to Unallocated). That filter is separate from the Programme filter's Unallocated, which also matches requests charged to an event on Unallocated.

## Claim for others

A reimbursement belongs to its claimant (`reimbursement.user_id`): they see it, are notified and are paid. `reimbursement.created_by` (migration 0144, null on older rows) records who raised it.

- **Permission**: `requests.create_for_others` (finance admins and super admins; excluded from admins). The reimbursement form shows a "Claim for" picker (`claim-for-field.tsx`, `claimant-picker.tsx`, list from `getClaimantsForPicker`: active, non-external users) only to holders, and only when creating a reimbursement.
- **Bank details**: for someone else the request is paid to one of the claimant's saved accounts, chosen by ID (`claimantBankAccountId`); the server copies its details and refuses another person's account or none when the claimant has accounts. A claimant with no saved account can't be claimed for. `bankAccount.bankAccountsForClaimant` reads another person's accounts only with the permission.
- **Change claimant**: `reimbursement.changeClaimant` (detail page menu, `change-claimant-dialog.tsx`) moves a request to another claimant at any status with a required reason, guarded by the claimant the client saw. Bank details switch to one of the new claimant's accounts (cleared when they have none), voucher and signed-copy links are cleared and an approved request queues fresh vouchers, since vouchers print the claimant as "Claimed by" (the voucher job also discards a render whose claimant changed meanwhile). History records "Raised by X for Y" or the change and reason; `notify-reimbursement-raised-for-you` and `notify-reimbursement-claimant-changed` tell the people involved. The audit ledger keeps only the user and account IDs and field names, never the reason.
- **Books**: a reimbursement's payout names its claimant ("Title (paid to Name)"; claimants have no party), so `changeClaimant` queues `ledger-post-source` like any edit and a posted request is reversed and reposted to the new claimant (layer 3-3a). Renaming the login doesn't repost on its own; the name catches up with the request's next repost.

## Books (layer 3-1)

**Tables** (migration `0132_ledger_books`, all in `packages/db/src/schema/ledger.ts`, synced read-only through Zero to `finance.view` and `finance.manage` only, `packages/zero/src/queries/ledger.ts`):

`fiscal_year.closed_by` joins `closed_at`. `org_account_kind` gains `deposit` (fixed deposit: no donations, no statement imports, money only by transfer; contributions and income imports refuse it) and `account_head_type` gains `fund` (fund balances; the entry's `fund_id` says which fund).

**Immutability.** The engine has no update or delete path, and the database refuses one too, so scripts, ad-hoc SQL and future code can't bypass it. Migration `0133_ledger_append_only` added the first triggers; `0134_ledger_guards` (the same SQL as `packages/db/src/ledger/immutability-sql.ts`, checked by a unit test) is the full set: no UPDATE or DELETE on `posting`, `ledger_entry`, `account_movement` and `movement_allocation`; none on `fiscal_year_event` except its actor becoming NULL when that login is deleted; none on `opening_balance` except the one-time reversal stamp and a creator or reverser becoming NULL; and a statement-level BEFORE TRUNCATE guard on all six. A reviewed repair or the retention process switches them off for its own transaction with `SET LOCAL pi_dash.ledger_maintenance = 'on'`. Databases built with `drizzle-kit push` (CI E2E, isolated worktrees) get the same guards from `ensureLedgerTriggers()`, which runs with the finance seeds and on every server start and does nothing when they are already in place. Foreign keys into the books are `restrict`. `ledger_entry.event_id` and `party_id` are therefore `restrict` too. Layer 3-2 posts a party (the donor) but no event (a contribution has no event); deleting a donor with gifts was already refused by `contribution.party_id`, so nothing changes for parties. Layer 3-3a keeps `event_id` restrict: an expense charged to an event names it for good, reversals included, so the event can't go. Events are deleted only with their team, and the team delete refuses with "The books hold expenses for this team's events, so the team can't be deleted." A vendor is the party of its expenses; deleting a vendor whose party the books name refuses with "The books hold expenses for this vendor, so it can't be deleted". Requests and payments cascade with the login that raised or recorded them, so deleting a user whose reimbursements, vendor payment requests or payments stand in the books refuses with "The books hold expenses this person raised or paid, so the account can't be deleted. Ban it instead." (`userHasExpensesInBooks`); otherwise the next posting run would reverse money that really left the bank.

**Engine** (`packages/db/src/ledger/`, server only):

- `post(tx, { sourceType, sourceId, sourceRevision?, action, economicOn, entries[], movements[] }, { actorId, now? })` returns `{ posting, created }`. It validates (`rules.ts`: amounts are safe integers above zero, dates real and not before the cutover `LEDGER_CUTOVER_ON` = 2026-04-01, transfer legs share a `transfer_id`, touch two accounts and net to zero), takes a transaction advisory lock per source, returns the existing posting for a repeated key, resolves every date to its fiscal period, reads the years `FOR SHARE` and refuses a closed year, and sets `posted_on` to today in India. A source that is reposted after a reversal (a corrected document) must bump `source_revision`, or the key returns the reversed posting; opening balances avoid this because each corrected document has its own ID.
- `reverse(tx, postingId, { reason, actorId })` appends a `reverse:<action>` posting with every entry's effect and every movement's direction flipped, on the original dates, linked through `reverses_*`. A reversal into a closed year is refused; a reversal can't itself be reversed.
- `opening-balance.ts`: `buildOpeningPosting` and `postOpeningBalance` / `reverseOpeningBalance`. Account lines become `opening` movements into the accounts; fixed assets, liabilities and fund balances become `increase` entries under the asset (`Fixed assets`), liability (`Audit fees payable`) and fund (`Fund balance`) heads, assets and liabilities in the General Fund. It posts only when account balances plus assets equal funds plus liabilities, only for the cutover year, and only once until reversed.
- `year-close.ts`: the checklist, `closeYear` and `reopenYear` (reason of at least five characters). Closing is blocked until the year has ended, the previous year the books keep (ending on or after the cutover) is closed, and, in the cutover year, the opening balances are posted. Reopening is refused while a later year is closed. Also blocking from layer 3-2: no record dated in the year waiting to post (`ledger_source_state`, by reason). Warnings for the treasurer to judge: bank statements imported to the year end, and each clearing account's balance at year end (shown, never failed: payouts in transit are normal). Only the year closes; monthly periods stay for dating.
- `reference-data.ts`: the Corpus Fund (restricted) and the heads (`Fixed assets`, `Audit fees payable`, `Fund balance`, `Interest on fixed deposit`, and from layer 3-2 `Donations received`, `Corpus donations` (a fund head, for corpus gifts), `In-kind donations`, `Other income`, `In-kind consumed`, `Gateway charges`, and from layer 3-3a the `Expenses` parent and `Supplier advances`), inserted by fixed ID on every server start and by the seeds, then checked. A row another row's code blocks is logged loudly with the code, posting refuses with "Books reference data missing", and the opening balances page says so. Not a migration, because PostgreSQL can't use the new `fund` enum value in the transaction that adds it. The account-head mutator refuses changing the type of, or deactivating, a reference head or any head a ledger entry posts under.

**Permissions** (`packages/db/src/permissions.ts`): `finance.post_opening`, `finance.close_year`, `finance.reopen_year`, `finance.adjust` (defined now; the adjustment document comes with layer 3-3), and from layer 3-2 `finance.post_pending` (Books → Post pending). Every finance admin and super admin holds them (founder decision: a user has one role, so there is no separate Treasurer role); `admin` does not. Reading the books needs `finance.view` or `finance.manage`; the opening balances page and the funds, account heads, organisation accounts and fiscal years it reads also admit `finance.post_opening`.

**Audit.** `apps/web/src/functions/books.ts` audits `books.opening_balance.post` (line counts per kind), `books.opening_balance.reverse`, `books.year.close` and `books.year.reopen`, with the document or year ID only; amounts, names and reasons stay in the books. The year-close checklist is a read.

**UI.** `/finance/books` (`finance.view` or `finance.manage`; `?year=`, `?account=`, `?tab=cash|income|balance`): a year select, the accounts with balances as of the year end or today, and three tabs: Cash book (one account's movements grouped by month, ordered by date then by their time-ordered ID, with a running balance; opening movements in the year count towards the opening balance, not money in), Income & expenditure (by account head or programme, `?by=`, with each line's entries in a sheet), and Balance summary (assets; funds, each carrying the income less expenditure posted to it, with its opening balance at the year's start and the current year's surplus or deficit beneath, and for the Corpus Fund its additions during the year (the corpus gifts credited to it, less their refunds, with any surplus posted to it); liabilities; and a "Balances" check). The header menu closes the year (`finance.close_year`, checklist dialog; not offered for years that haven't started) or reopens it (`finance.reopen_year`, reason). `/finance/books/opening` shows the posted opening balances read-only to book readers, with Reverse for `finance.post_opening`, or the entry form for `finance.post_opening` when none stand: the accounts, fixed assets one line per asset, liabilities, the funds, a live "Assets = funds + liabilities" check, and the CA's name, date and confirmation. The evidence upload of the signed statements is not built yet.

## Income posting (layer 3-2)

Layer 3-2 turns income records into postings on the 3-1 engine. Server only: the browser never posts. Every rule the CA may change lives in one module, `packages/db/src/ledger/income-rules.ts` (pure functions of a source's facts, unit-tested); `income-posting.ts` loads the facts, compares the result with the postings standing for the source and reverses and reposts only what differs; `income-runner.ts` runs it against the app database. The rules (founder answers of 14 October 2026; the CA still has to confirm them, `finance-ledger-cutover.md` section 3):

| Source (`posting.source_type`) | Recognition (`recognise` part) | Cash book (`cash` part) | Dated |
|---|---|---|---|
| `contribution`, gateway channel (Razorpay, give.do), status received, receipted or refunded (pending and reversed gifts don't post) | `Donations received` at the full amount, with the gift's fund, programme, project and donor; Razorpay only: fee plus GST as `Gateway charges` | gross into the gift's clearing account (`receipt`); Razorpay only: fee plus GST out (`fee`) | received on |
| `contribution`, UPI, NEFT, cheque (and cash, should one occur) | `Donations received` | into the gift's account (`receipt`) | received on |
| `contribution`, donation type corpus, any money channel | `Corpus donations` (a fund head, not income) at the full amount, always in the Corpus Fund, with the gift's donor; Razorpay only: fee plus GST as `Gateway charges` in the General Fund | as for its channel | received on |
| `contribution`, in kind | `In-kind donations` and an equal `In-kind consumed`, both medium `in_kind` (first release: in-kind as consumed) | none | received on |
| `contribution_refund` | `Donations received` decrease with the gift's dimensions (`Corpus donations` in the Corpus Fund for a corpus gift) | out of the gift's account (`refund`), clearing for a gateway gift | refunded on |
| `other_income` | `Interest on fixed deposit` for kind interest or anything credited to a fixed deposit, else `Other income` | into its account (`receipt`) | received on |
| `gateway_settlement`, processed | instant settlement fee plus GST as `Gateway charges` (General Fund); give.do: each linked gift's charges as `Gateway charges` with that gift's dimensions (General Fund for a corpus gift) | net out of clearing and into the bank (`transfer`, `transfer_id` = the settlement); the fees out of clearing (`fee`) | the matched bank line's date, else settled on |
| `statement_transfer` (a bank line marked as a transfer, with the other account picked) | none | out of one account, into the other (`transfer`, `transfer_id` = the line) | the line's date |

- **Cutover.** Nothing dated before 1 April 2026 posts, except a gateway gift captured earlier whose processed payout reached the bank on or after it: it posts on that payout's bank day as FY 2026-27 income, with its fee, so the clearing account opens and stays at zero. A gift captured on 30 March and paid out on 2 April posts on 2 April; one paid out on 31 March never posts. A refund on or after the cutover posts even when its gift was received before it (a reduction of FY 2026-27 income). A gateway refund made before the cutover that a processed payout reaching the bank on or after it took back posts on that payout's bank day, mirroring the gift's exception (founder rule, CA to confirm): a gift of 30 March, refunded on 31 March and paid out net on 2 April, posts gift, fee and refund on 2 April and the clearing account ends at zero.
- **Corpus gifts are capital receipts** (founder decision, 16 October 2026). A gift of donation type corpus (the donor's written direction that it form part of the corpus) is credited straight to the Corpus Fund under `Corpus donations`, a `fund` head, so it never passes through the I&E and stays out of the year's surplus; the type decides the fund, so a gift recorded against another fund before the type-to-fund rule is still credited to the Corpus Fund. Its gateway charges (Razorpay at the gift, give.do at the payout) are `Gateway charges` in the General Fund, so the Corpus Fund keeps the gross the donor gave and the General Fund bears the cost; a refund reduces the Corpus Fund. In-kind corpus gifts keep the in-kind rule (in-kind income and an equal consumption, both in the Corpus Fund, netting to nothing; CA to advise). The balance views show the year's corpus gifts as the Corpus Fund's additions during the year; later years carry them in its opening.
- **Rule changes reach posted sources.** `INCOME_RULES_VERSION` in `income-rules.ts` is bumped when a rule changes how sources already posted should stand (1: the corpus rules above). While the version recorded in `app_config` (`ledger_income_rules_version`) is older, the scheduled run and Books → Post pending look at every source instead of only changed ones, so a source posted under the old rule is reversed and reposted (the recognition only, when the money is unchanged) on the next run; once a run over everything finishes with nothing failed or left, the version is recorded. A source dated in a closed year is left alone: its reversal is refused, so it waits ("Year closed") and its books stand as posted until the year is reopened, when the next run reposts it.
- **No TDS, no cash.** The trust deducts no TDS and keeps no cash; CSR gifts sit in the General Fund like other donations (founder answers). give.do's fee filled on a contribution from the payout file is never posted at the gift: give.do charges post with the payout.
- **Payout's bank account.** The matched bank statement line's account; else the account the payout is already posted to (so adding a bank account later moves nothing); else the single active bank account; with several and no match, the payout waits ("Pick the bank account: match the payout's bank statement line").
- **Razorpay fee recorded nowhere on the gift.** A gift recorded by hand and linked to its Razorpay payment later has no fee; its payout line's fee and GST then post with the payout, so clearing still nets to zero.
- **Lines the books can't use** (settlement adjustments and transfers, payment lines linked to no gift, refund lines linked to no refund) are not posted; they are counted in `ledger_source_state.attention_count` and shown as "payout lines need a look" on Books and on the settlement sheet.
- **Transfers between own accounts.** Mark as transfer on a bank line now asks for the other account (an active bank account or the fixed deposit; stored in `statement_line.transfer_org_account_id`, migration `0135_ledger_income`). A debit posts out of the line's account into the other; a credit the reverse. Between two bank accounts, each with its own statement, marking the second side pairs the two lines one-to-one (`statement_line.transfer_pair_line_id` on both, under the statement line lock): the other bank's marked, not yet paired line in the opposite direction, same amount and a value date within five days, the nearest in date, then the first marked. The line marked first posts both legs (either side, so a transfer marked only on the receiving bank's credit still posts); its pair posts nothing; a marked line with no pair posts its own transfer. Two equal transfers therefore never share a counterpart. Unmatching a line clears its other account and the pair on both sides, and reverses the transfer ("The bank line was unmatched"). New marks need the other account (the server refuses one without); a line marked before this layer, with no other account, waits ("Pick the other account"); unmatch and mark it again.
- **Edits.** A change that touches only the recognition (programme, project, fund, donor, donation type, including a gift made or unmade corpus) reverses and reposts the `recognise` part alone, so a classification correction never moves money. Any change to the money (amount, date, account, channel, a newly matched bank line) reverses and reposts both parts. A change to a payment reference alone (UTR, payment ID) reposts nothing; the next real repost carries the new reference. A deleted record, or one that no longer posts (back to pending, unmatched), is reversed. Each repost takes the next `source_revision`; reversals carry a reason ("The contribution changed", "The contribution was deleted").
- **Closed year.** A posting or reversal into a closed year is refused by the engine; the record waits with reason "Year closed" and posts after the year is reopened (the reopen queues a sweep).
- **Year close.** Closing the year first posts that year's records (for up to 15 seconds); the checklist itself is a read and never posts. The "waiting to post" check counts waiting records dated in the year and records dated in it that no posting run has looked at yet ("Not looked at yet"). Posting a source takes the year-close lock shared and closing takes it exclusively, so no record can start waiting between the check and the close.

**Running it.** After an income record changes, the change's own path queues a `ledger-post-source` job (`{ sourceType, sourceId }`, singleton per source, policy `stately`) after the commit, with `withFireAndForgetLog`: the contribution and other income mutators (Zero async tasks), the bank line actions (the payout or transfer the line was and is tied to), and the Razorpay payment, refund and settlement syncs (looked up by the provider's IDs, `packages/db/src/queries/ledger-sources.ts`). A source job also posts the sources that follow it: a gift's refunds and processed payouts (their charges carry its dimensions), a payout's pre-cutover gifts and refunds (they post on its bank day), and the other side of a bank-to-bank transfer. A Razorpay payment or refund sync also queues the processed payouts whose lines it linked. Bulk paths queue the `ledger-post-pending` sweep instead: contribution and give.do payout imports, a Razorpay reconcile run, a bank statement import or "Find matches" that matched lines. The sweep runs daily at 03:30 India time and is the backfill from 1 April: it lists every contribution, refund, other income and settlement in the posting window, gateway gifts and refunds settled by a processed payout, transfer lines, every source with standing postings whose record is gone and every waiting source, and posts each in its own transaction (safe to rerun: an up-to-date source writes nothing; one failure never holds up the rest). The daily run and Post pending are incremental: each look stores in `ledger_source_state.source_updated_at` when the record, and what it depends on (the gift behind a refund or payout line, the account other income went into, the payout's matched bank line, the bank accounts, a transfer's pair), last changed, and the sweep skips the source until that moves. Matching or unmatching a payout's bank line and linking its payment or refund lines touch `gateway_settlement.updated_at` for this; sources with no state row, waiting ones, pre-cutover gateway records and transfer lines are always looked at. The Sunday run looks at everything. The queue is `singleton` with a heartbeat and no retry, and a run stops itself after 45 minutes, so an expiry never starts a second sweep alongside a live one. Jobs are outside the audit ledger.

**State.** `ledger_source_state` (one row per source, updated in place, not under the ledger's guards; synced read-only to book readers) holds `posted` with the day the latest posting was made, `waiting` with a reason (`year_closed`, `pick_bank_account`, `no_clearing_account`, `pick_other_account`, `reference_data_missing`, or `error` with the engine's message) and the date it would post on, or `none` with why nothing posts (`before_cutover`, `not_received`, `not_processed`, `other_side_posts`). A deleted source's row is removed once its postings are reversed.

**UI.** Books shows a "Waiting to post" banner when anything waits or payout lines need a look, with a sheet listing each record (linking to it), its date and reason. "Post pending" (header menu and banner, `finance.post_pending`, audited as `books.post_pending` with the waiting count) runs the sweep for up to 20 seconds in the request and hands the rest to the job. Cash book rows and income and expenditure entries link to their source: a contribution or refund opens the gift's sheet (`/income?contribution=` or `?refund=`), a settlement its sheet (`/income?tab=settlements&settlement=`), other income its tab, a transfer the bank statements. Contribution and settlement sheets show "In the books: posted 16 Oct 2026", or waiting and why, to book readers.

## Expense posting (layer 3-3a)

Layer 3-3a turns approved expenses into postings on the same engine and posting run as income. Server only. The rules live in one module, `packages/db/src/ledger/expense-rules.ts` (pure functions, unit-tested, each quoting the posting table of `finance-ledger-cutover.md` section 3); `expense-posting.ts` loads a source's facts and gives the shared run (`income-posting.ts`) its watermark, listing and related sources, so comparing, reversing, reposting, waiting and the sweep are exactly those of income.

| Source (`posting.source_type`) | Recognition (`recognise`) | Cash book (`cash`) | Dated |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reimbursement`, approved, with the payment screenshot | one expense entry per line item, under the line's category head, with the request's programme, project and event from Charged to (`expenseProgrammeOf`), the General Fund, the line's description (else the title) as memo | the total out of the main bank account (`payout`), memo the title and the claimant ("Class outing (paid to Ravi)") | the approval day in India (the payment date; pi-dash records no separate one) |
| `vendor_payment`, invoice or no-invoice reason approved (status `completed`) | one expense entry per line item at its full amount (recoverable GST is nil: the trust is not GST-registered), dimensions from Charged to, the vendor as party; and an equal `Supplier advances` decrease, clearing what its approved payments advanced; it waits while the lines are more than the approved payments dated from the cutover | none (the money left with each payment) | the day the invoice was approved in India, else the invoice date |
| `vendor_payment_transaction`, approved, on a request that is approved or later | a `Supplier advances` increase (asset), the vendor as party, General Fund | out of the main bank account (`payout`), with the payment reference, memo the request's title | the payment's date |

- **Main bank account.** "Out of the main bank account (the trust deals in no cash)" (cutover doc section 3). pi-dash records no account on a payment, so a paid expense leaves the account its standing posting already used, else the bank account marked **Main bank account** in Settings → Organisation accounts (`org_account.is_main`, migration `0145_expense_posting`: at most one, only an active bank account; marking another unmarks the old one, deactivating it unmarks it), else the only active bank account. With several and none marked, it waits ("mark the main bank account"). Matching the bank debit comes with layer 3-3b.
- **Supplier advances (decision).** The cutover table says a confirmed vendor payment recognises "Nothing (already recognised)", assuming the invoice comes first; in pi-dash the invoice always comes after payment (`vendor-payments.md`). So each payment is a supplier advance until its invoice is accepted, as section 3.6 says ("Payments before the invoice are supplier advances until allocated"), under the `Supplier advances` asset head (reference data), and the accepted invoice clears it. Without this the balance summary is off by every paid request whose invoice is still pending. The invoice clears only its own amount, so the posting always balances: when the payments were more (an overpayment), the difference stays on the vendor's advance for the CA to look at; when the lines are more than the approved payments (lines raised, or a payment rejected or deleted, after the invoice), the invoice waits with "The invoice is more than the approved payments" until they agree. A request whose approved payments are all dated before 1 April 2026 stays history (nothing posts for its invoice, as the opening balances hold that money); one paid on both sides of the cutover waits for the CA ("Paid partly before 1 Apr 2026").
- **Account heads.** Expense categories become account heads under one `Expenses` parent (section 7: "These become account heads under one expense parent"): `expense_category.account_head_id` (migration `0145_expense_posting`) is filled by `ensureExpenseCategoryHeads` (`expense-heads.ts`), which creates one expense head per category, named after it with code `EXPENSE_<NAME>`, on every server start, in the seeds, and in the posting run for a category added since. The CA's Tally names go on the heads; renaming a category doesn't rename its head. The `Expenses` parent (`LEDGER_ACCOUNT_HEAD_IDS.expenses`) once shared the Corpus donations head's ID, so databases made then have no Expenses row and their category heads sit under Corpus donations; `repairExpensesParent` (`reference-data.ts`, run with the reference data on every server start and in the seeds) gives that row back to Corpus donations' code, name and type when it holds no entries, creates Expenses under its own ID, and moves the `EXPENSE_*` heads under it. Postings were never affected: expense lines post to the category heads, never to their parent. A category pointed at an asset head posts an asset ("or an asset for equipment"); there is no screen for that mapping yet.
- **Waiting.** An approved reimbursement without a payment screenshot (older records) waits with "No payment screenshot", so it is on the Books page's Waiting to post list, the founder's list of section 6. One with its screenshot but no approval day recorded waits with "The approval date isn't recorded" (`no_approval_day`); one whose lines add up to zero posts nothing, "Nothing to post: the total is zero" (`nothing_to_post`). A paid vendor payment whose invoice isn't approved shows "The invoice isn't approved yet" on its state and posts no expense; its payments post.
- **Payment proof after approval.** `reimbursement.addPaymentProof` (needs `requests.approve`, the permission that attaches the screenshot at approval) adds the payment screenshot to an approved, unvoided reimbursement that has none, or replaces it. It claims the upload with `claimUploadedR2ObjectKey` into the same place approval does (`approval_screenshot_key`, `reimbursements/<id>/approval-screenshots`), so posting needs no special case, and queues `ledger-post-source` after the commit (`reimbursement:<id>` singleton key), so the waiting claim posts on its approval day. Replacing never deletes evidence: the old screenshot becomes a plain reimbursement attachment ("payment-proof-replaced-<day>.<ext>", the day in India, with "-2" and on for a repeat), as it does when a re-approval brings a new screenshot, and a reset to pending keeps it as "payment-proof-previous-<day>.<ext>"; kept, only approvers can remove it in the edit form; the client says whether it saw a screenshot (`replacing`), so two adds never race. History says "Payment proof added" or "Payment proof replaced"; the audit ledger records the reimbursement ID, the kept attachment ID and field names only. On the web: Add or Replace payment proof in the reimbursement's ⋯ menu, a "Payment proof" card with Add payment proof on an approved claim without one (shown to approvers only), a "Payment proof → Missing payment proof" filter and a "Missing payment proof · N" count on Reimbursements (approved, not void, no screenshot, and approved from 1 Apr 2026 in India or with no approval date, `lib/payment-proof.ts`: the same claims the Books list shows as waiting for one), and on the Books Waiting to post list a count of claims without proof linking to that filter and an "Add payment proof" link on each such row (`/reimbursements/<id>?proof=add` opens the dialog). Vendor payments need no such action: their posting never waits for a screenshot (a payment posts on its approval and date; the request's screenshot is optional, `vendor-payments.md`).
- **Cutover.** Nothing approved, accepted or paid before 1 April 2026 posts (each source by the day it posts on).
- **Not built here.** TDS posts nothing (the trust deducts none). The audit fee: the founder's answer is to treat the FY 2025-26 audit fee's payment as FY 2026-27 expense, so it posts as any vendor payment charged to the organisation; writing back the opening `Audit fees payable` liability is the CA's open question and would be an adjustment (layer 3-3c). Transfers between the current account and the FD already post from a bank line marked as a transfer (layer 3-2).
- **Corrections.** An edit after posting (lines, amounts, the event's programme, a reimbursement's claimant), an un-approval (reset to pending, a reopened invoice review), a rejected or deleted payment and a deleted request reverse and repost (or only reverse) by source revision, as for income. A re-charge before posting reposts the recognition only; after posting `setChargedTo` refuses it.
- **Voiding (Option B, founder decision).** A reimbursement, vendor payment request or vendor payment the books hold is never deleted: `delete` refuses it on the server with "Posted to the books. Void it instead." (`assertDeletable` in `packages/zero/src/mutators/void.ts`, which reads the standing posting; a vendor payment request is refused when the request or any of its payments stands). Drafts, pending and rejected requests and anything without a standing posting delete as before. Instead, `reimbursement.void`, `vendorPayment.void` and `vendorPaymentTransaction.void` take a reason (1 to 500 characters) and need `requests.void` (finance admins and super admins). Voiding sets `voided_at`, `voided_by` and `void_reason` (migration `0146_void_requests`; a check keeps the time and reason together) and keeps the status it had, the request, its attachments, vouchers and history; it writes a `voided` history entry with the reason, and the audit ledger records the action and entity ID only. The columns sit beside the status rather than as a `void` status because the status machines (three of them, read by approvals, payments, invoices, notifications and the posting rules) stay as they were, a void can come at any status, and the voided record still says what it was (approved, paid), as `contribution_receipt` does with its void columns. Voiding a vendor payment request voids each of its payments too; a voided payment no longer counts as paid (`recalculateParentStatus`, the payment cap and the invoice's cleared advance). Voiding twice changes nothing. A voided record takes no further change: every transition (edit, approve, reject, reset, charge, claimant, invoice steps, voucher generation, recording or approving payments) refuses with "This is void, so it can't be changed", and a voided record can't be deleted. In the books, the rules return nothing for a voided source with `reverseOn` the void day in India, and the posting run reverses what stands with `reverse(…, { on })`: the reversal posting, its entries and movements are dated the void day (never before the original), in the void day's year, so only that year must be open; while it is closed the source waits ("The year is closed") like any posting. A posting is reversed at most once (`posting_reverses_uidx`), so a second run or the sweep reverses nothing more. On the web, lists of reimbursements and vendor payments show a "Void" badge, hide voided rows until "Show voided · N" is pressed, and leave voided rows out of the stats; on a record the books hold (`ledger.postedSources`, `ledger.sourceState`) the row menu and the detail page offer Void instead of Delete; a voided reimbursement's vouchers stay (their numbers stay used: `voucher_seq` never reuses one) and are marked VOID, and the voucher job makes no new one for it.
- **Running it.** The reimbursement, vendor payment and vendor payment transaction mutators queue `ledger-post-source` for the record after the commit (`packages/zero/src/mutators/ledger-queue.ts`); a vendor payment's job posts its payments after it. The daily sweep and Post pending list approved reimbursements, completed vendor payments and approved payments from the cutover, and the year close counts them. Books cash book and income and expenditure rows link to the request (a payment opens its request), the request's Charged to card shows "In the books", and the CA workbook names a reimbursement payout's programme and head from its recognition.

## Donation receipts (layer 4)

**Policy** (founder, 14 October 2026; `packages/shared/src/receipts.ts`, shown read-only in Settings → Organisation accounts → Donation receipts): Razorpay Payment Pages and give.do send their own 80G receipts (give.do under the trust's registration), so pi-dash never receipts `razorpay` or `give_do` gifts. pi-dash receipts direct gifts (`upi`, `neft`, `cheque`, and `cash` should it happen) received on or after `RECEIPTING_START_DAY` = 2026-04-01; earlier gifts were receipted outside pi-dash. In-kind gifts are not receipted. The CA's all-gifts file of the donations return shows each gift's issuer and pi-dash receipt number.

**Tables** (migration `0136_donation_receipts`): `contribution_receipt` (public, synced read-only through Zero to `finance.view`, `finance.manage`, `donors.manage` and `donors.receipt`): contribution, financial-year label of the issue date, sequence, number (`PI/2026-27/0001`, unique), India issue date, issuer, `voided_at`/`voided_by`/`void_reason`, `emailed_at`. A partial unique index allows one receipt per gift that is not voided, so a voided gift can be receipted again under a new number (decision: the brief's "contribution_id unique" read as "one live receipt"). Server-only in schema `private`: `contribution_receipt_counter` (last sequence per financial year) and `contribution_receipt_snapshot` (what the PDF prints, frozen at issue: donor name and address, ID type, gift facts, the trust's particulars, the 80G registration and signatory, as jsonb; the donor's ID number re-encrypted with `DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY` bound to the receipt ID). A later edit to the donor or the settings never changes an issued receipt.

**Numbering.** One gap-free series per financial year of the issue date: a gift received on 31 March and receipted on 2 April takes the new year's number, and its PDF prints both dates (receipt date and date received). Issuing increments the year's counter row with `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE … RETURNING` inside the issuing transaction; the row lock queues parallel issues and a rollback gives the number back (no Postgres `SEQUENCE`, which skips on rollback). Numbers are never reused: voiding keeps the receipt and its number with the reason.

**Eligibility** (`assessReceipt`): a direct gift on or after the start date, status `received`, no refund recorded (any refund blocks the receipt, since even a partial one would make the stated amount wrong), INR, the donor not the GiveIndia platform party, with a name and an address with PIN code. The receipt carries the 80G line unless the gift is marked Exclude, the donor opted out of the tax certificate (gift on Follow donor), or it is cash above ₹2,000; with the line the donor also needs a valid ID number, printed in full on the PDF only. Without the line the receipt has no ID and says no deduction certificate is issued (decision). The trust's name, address, PAN and signatory (`donation_receipt_settings` in `app_config`, `finance.manage`) and the 80G registration (`donations_return_registration`) must be set before anything is issued.

**Deleting.** A gift any receipt was issued for, voided ones included, can't be deleted ("A receipt was issued for this gift; it can't be deleted"); the delete mutator refuses and the UI hides Delete. Receipts are read only by holders of an income permission (`canReadReceipts`), so `donors.receipt` alone opens nothing.

**Commands** (`apps/web/src/functions/contribution-receipts.ts`, `donors.receipt`, a new permission held by finance admins and super admins only): issue one or many (`contribution_receipt.issue`: requested count, then issued and skipped counts and up to 50 issued gift IDs added when it finishes; the gifts are locked, re-assessed, numbered in received-date order and set to `receipted` in one transaction, changed gifts skipped and reported), void with a reason of at least five characters that holds no PAN- or Aadhaar-shaped text (`contribution_receipt.void`; the gift returns to `received`), and email (`contribution_receipt.email`). Download is `GET /api/receipts/$receiptId` (`donors.receipt`, audited `contribution_receipt.download` because the PDF carries the full ID; a missing receipt is audited as a failure). Receipts have no ledger effect.

**PDF** (`packages/pdf/src/donation-receipt.tsx`, rendered on demand, never stored; `packages/jobs/src/lib/receipt-pdf.ts`): the trust's name, address, PAN, 80G URN and date; number and date; donor name, address and ID; amount in figures and Indian words (`packages/shared/src/amount-in-words.ts`); mode with reference, date received, donation wording, programme or project; the 80G line and the Form 114 note (wording in shared constants marked CA TO CONFIRM); the signatory block. Voided receipts download marked VOID with the reason. The file is named after the number with slashes replaced.

**Email** is manual only (Email receipt): the server refuses a voided receipt, a donor without an email address, and an address on the suppression list, then enqueues `send-contribution-receipt` (receipt and request IDs only). The job checks again, renders the PDF and the `DonationReceiptEmail` template, sends through Resend with the PDF attached and a per-request idempotency key, and stamps `emailed_at` when sent (not when Resend is unconfigured). The PDF renders byte-for-byte the same on a retry, and a Resend 409 (key already used) is recorded as already sent.

**UI.** Income → Receipts (receipt readers): number, issue date, donor, amount, status (Issued/Voided), emailed; a row opens a sheet with Download, Email and Void for issuers. "Issue receipts (N)" (the Receipts tab's primary action, or the Income menu) opens a review listing what will be issued and what is blocked and why, with an optional "Received up to" date to issue in tranches (at most 500 a time). The count loads only on the Receipts tab. The contribution sheet's Receipt line reads "Receipt PI/… · Download", "Receipted by Razorpay/give.do", or "Not receipted (reason)", with Issue receipt for a single ready gift. On the Contributions table, Status shows the money's lifecycle only (Pending, Received, Refunded, Reversed; a stored `receipted` shows and filters as Received, its edit lock unchanged), and a Receipt column with a matching filter (receipt readers only) shows the receipt number (a download link for issuers), By Razorpay/By give.do, Ready to issue, Needs details (only the donor's name, address or ID holds it up), Can't issue (something on the gift does: a refund, foreign currency, a platform recorded as donor, a status mismatch; the reasons in a tooltip), Not needed (in kind, before the start date) or a dash (pending, refunded, reversed), sorted in that order: `receiptColumnValue` in `packages/shared/src/receipts.ts`. The browser works it out from synced data except the split of open direct gifts, which turns on donors' private details: `getContributionReceiptReadiness` (receipt readers, unaudited read; `loadReceiptReadiness`, one bounded query with `NOT EXISTS` a live receipt, decrypting ID numbers only where the 80G line needs them) returns gift IDs and block codes only. The table loads it once Zero has the contributions, then debounced, never overlapping, skipped while the tab is hidden, on contribution changes and when the tab is shown again; a row the answer misses triggers one refetch, and a failure shows Unavailable with Retry. Saved Status filters for Receipted read as Received.

## CA handoff workbook (layer 3-5)

Finance → Books → ⋯ → **Download CA workbook (XLSX)** builds one workbook for the selected year, `proud-indian-ca-workbook-<FY>.xlsx`, with the sheets and columns of `finance-ledger-cutover.md` section 5 (XLSX only, pi-dash head names as they are). It reads the posted books as they stand, reversals included, so a correction shows as the original, its reversal and the repost. The menu item is disabled, with the reason underneath, for years before FY 2026-27 and until the cutover year's opening balances are posted; the server refuses the same.

- **Server.** `exportCaWorkbook({ fiscalYearId })` in `apps/web/src/functions/books.ts` (`finance.view` or `finance.manage`), audited as `books.ca_workbook.export` with the year, the row counts and whether the donor list was included. Everything is read in one read-only, repeatable-read transaction (`readWorkbookSnapshot`, `packages/db/src/queries/ca-workbook.ts`), so the sheets agree: the year's movements and entries row by row, everything before the year summed in SQL into brought-forward rows (the 100,000-row bound counts the year's rows only), and the donations return's gifts and receipts. Sheet rows are pure functions in `apps/web/src/lib/ca-workbook/sheets.ts`, written in memory by `xlsx.ts` with `write-excel-file` (server only; its one dependency, `fflate`, was already in the lockfile) and returned base64 to the browser.
- **Sheets.** Receipts and payments (per account: balance brought forward, each movement signed, balance carried forward; party, programme, project and head from the source's standing recognition of the matching kind when they agree (a receipt or refund matches income or, for a corpus gift, its `Corpus donations` line), so payouts and transfers name none; "Bank statement matched" Yes or No on bank accounts, counting the paired line of a bank-to-bank transfer for both legs, n/a elsewhere; other income has no statement link yet, so it shows No); Income and expenditure (every recognition entry of the year, including the opening asset, liability and fund lines, decreases negative, the head name in "Account head" and the line's memo in a last "Memo" column); Income and expenditure summary (head by programme, grouped by ID, with per head, per section and per programme totals and the surplus, then the year's capital receipts apart, "Corpus donations (Corpus Fund)" net of refunds, which are not income); Balance sheet items (each account; each fixed asset opening line on its own, with later entries under that head in "Fixed assets — other"; each liability head; each fund, where a fund carrying income less expenditure shows as its opening balance (fund balance entries plus every earlier year's surplus) and the year's figure (the General Fund's current year surplus/(deficit); another fund's additions/(deductions) during the year, which for the Corpus Fund are its corpus gifts less their refunds, gross, plus any surplus posted to it) with the fund's total in bold, a fund zero throughout left out, as note 5 of the audited statements; from opening through the year to closing, bank and FD closings beside the last bank statement balance, and the check row); Donor list (Form 113: the donations return's all-gifts rows with the receipt issuer and number).
- **Funds carry the surplus.** There is no year-close posting: income less expenditure stays in the fund each entry is posted to (`ledger_entry.fund_id`; the General Fund for unrestricted income), corpus gifts are credited to the Corpus Fund directly (no surplus at all), and the balance sheet items and the Books balance summary read it there. A fund's opening at 1 April is its fund balance entries, earlier years' corpus gifts and every earlier year's surplus since the cutover; the year's surplus is its increase (a deficit its decrease), and the year's corpus gifts are the Corpus Fund's additions, so the funds' surpluses add up to the summary's surplus (corpus gifts are not in it) and the check row stays at zero. Those rows are net figures (the gross income and expenditure are in the I&E sheets); the General Fund's reads "current year surplus/(deficit)", any other fund's "additions/(deductions) during the year", and a fund that is zero throughout has no row. The next year therefore needs no opening-balance entry. Nothing is frozen at close: reopening a year and posting into it changes every later year's openings, so a later workbook already signed off can drift from a fresh download. The Books summary reads a fund's opening as its balance less the year's corpus gifts (`CAPITAL_RECEIPT_HEAD_IDS`) and surplus, so it assumes the `Fund balance` head is posted only by the opening balances; an in-year fund-balance posting would show inside the opening there, while the workbook shows it as the year's movement. The I&E sheets and the Books I&E tab are unchanged.
- **Formats.** Amounts are numbers in rupees with an INR format using Indian digit grouping (₹12,34,567.00); dates are Excel dates shown dd-mm-yyyy; the header row is bold and frozen.
- **Decisions.** Movement ID, Entry ID and Memo columns follow the contract's columns, so "Reversal of" can name an entry. Backfilled = the entry's economic date is before the day the ledger went live (the posting day of the cutover year's first opening balances; "No" until they are posted); a reversal takes its original's flag. Donor IDs are always masked to their last four characters; the full IDs go only in the Form 113 upload file. The donor list is included only for holders of `donors.view`, `donors.manage` or `finance.view_sensitive`; for other book readers the sheet is a one-line note. Every text cell starting with `= + - @`, a tab or a carriage return gets a leading apostrophe; XLSX never runs text, so this only protects a sheet saved as CSV, and the values the CA matches on (IDs, UTRs, receipt numbers) never start with those characters.

## give.do payout imports (layer 3f)

Migration `0129_give_do_payouts` adds the `give_do` settlement provider and `give_do_payout` import source. Income's Settlements action and menu open `/income/payout/import`, guarded by both `donors.manage` and `finance.manage`. The server reads an XLSX workbook with exact `Donations` and `Campaign Level` headers, capped at 5 MB and 5,000 rows. Before the XLSX reader runs, ZIP validation limits the archive to 1,000 entries, 25 MiB per entry, 50 MiB expanded overall and a 100:1 expansion ratio; bounded inflation also rejects forged sizes. ZIP64 and inconsistent archive records are refused. Numeric date cells use the workbook's 1900 or 1904 epoch, alongside formatted dates and DD/MM/YYYY text. Each file has one UTR and unique transaction IDs; donation gross equals charges plus payable, and every campaign's gross, charges and payable must agree between sheets. Non-INR or converted-currency rows fail with the shared FCRA guard. The bank and contribution importers remain CSV-only.

The review defaults Settled on to the upload's India date because the workbook has no payout date; the reviewer can use the email date instead. A processed settlement uses provider `give_do`, provider account `live`, external ID and UTR equal to the file's UTR, and the selected active clearing account. Each payment item stores Transaction ID, payable as credit, charges as fee and zero tax. The contribution identity retains the existing give.do convention: its provider account is the clearing account ID. A transaction already recorded under another clearing account, or with incompatible gross, channel or account, blocks the run for correction.

Known transactions link by payment identity. The reviewer may opt into importing unknown donations in the same transaction, using the existing GiveIndia intermediary, donor matching, fill-missing choices and collection events. Otherwise their settlement items remain unlinked; a later give.do contribution import links compatible items. Payout evidence fills a zero contribution fee; a different nonzero fee is preserved and shown as a mismatch. A Transaction ID cannot occur in another processed give.do payout. The shared import lock serializes runs and retention. Raw donation rows, keyed hashes, masking and 30/180-day retention use the existing import pipeline; audit metadata contains only counts and record IDs.

A repeated UTR never rewrites its settlement or creates donations. Changed totals or changed item evidence set the existing mismatch flag and preserve the original values. Give.do settlements participate in exact UTR bank matches and amount/date suggestions alongside the configured Razorpay mode; give.do narration remains a fallback suggestion. After a new payout commits, a separate statement transaction refreshes active bank accounts with eligible unmatched credits of the same net amount, acquiring the statement advisory lock before any contribution row locks. A failed refresh is logged without undoing the committed payout; the existing Find matches action can retry it. The Settlements tab lists both providers even when Razorpay is unconfigured, and shows awaiting amounts separately: received gifts without a processed payment item, net of their recorded fees and tax. A processed give.do settlement fixes the contribution's donor, amount, date, account and channel and prevents deletion.

The Income contribution table's `Reached bank` facet is available only to `finance.view` or `finance.manage`. It derives Yes from a processed settlement payment item or a matched contribution statement line; Not yet from an unsettled gateway gift or unmatched UPI, NEFT, cheque or cash gift with an account; Not applicable from in-kind or cash without an account. A finance-authorized server function loads evidence only for the table's contribution scope after its other filters, and only while Reached bank is in use. The filter waits for the complete current request before presenting derived results; no status is stored on contributions. This definition treats a processed gateway payout as reached bank even before its bank statement is matched.

Synthetic parser and E2E fixtures live under `packages/e2e/fixtures/give-do/`; regenerate the workbook with `bun packages/e2e/fixtures/give-do/generate.ts`. Never use a donor export as a fixture.

## 1. Why

Today pi-dash records three expense-request workflows (reimbursement, advance payment, vendor payment) with line items tagged by a flat expense category. Advance payments are no longer used; existing records stay read-only for history. There is no income, no fund, programme or period dimension, no budget, and no stored voucher register. The financial-year export decides the year from a different date per request type and drops vendor payments that have no invoice yet.

Year-end data is collected from Razorpay, give.do, UPI, bank statements, CSR agreements and pi-dash separately, then handed to the chartered accountant (CA), who consolidates and files. The donation statement (Form 113, formerly 10BD), donor certificates (Form 114, formerly 10BE) and, after FCRA registration, FC-4 all need data pi-dash does not hold.

Goal: pi-dash keeps the full operational financial record year-round, organised by programme, and produces exports that the CA, the Income Tax portal and the FCRA portal can take with minimal rework. Reports for learning (cost per beneficiary, budget vs actual, donor retention, volunteer effort) come from the same data.

## 3. Model

Documents are editable while pending. Submitting freezes a document revision. Approval, receipt or payment creates a **posting** that writes recognition entries and account movements in one server transaction. Recognition and cash totals come from their respective books; obligations and filings come from frozen source records and allocations.

```
parties ──┐
          ├─ documents (contribution, grant, reimbursement, vendor invoice, payout)
dimensions┘        │ submit → frozen revision → approve / receive / pay
                   ▼
            posting (idempotent per source action, period-checked)
              ├─ ledger_entry       recognition: income, expense, asset, with dimensions
              └─ account_movement   cash book: money in/out of org accounts
                   │
                   ▼
   reconciliation (statement lines ↔ movements), exports, filings, insights
```

### 3.6 Expense documents

Existing `reimbursement`, `vendor_payment` and `vendor_payment_transaction` stay, with these changes. `advance_payment` and its child tables become read-only: creation and edit mutators are removed, the UI shows them as a legacy list, and they take no part in postings.

- All amounts become `*_paise bigint`.
- Every line item references `account_head_id`, `fund_id`, `programme_id` (from the header; one programme per request), optional `project_id` and `event_id`, and carries `incurred_on`.
- Header gets `cost_centre_id`, `revision`, `submitted_at` and `supersedes_id`. Submitting freezes the revision. A financial edit before posting bumps the revision and invalidates outstanding approvals. After posting, a correction is a new document with `supersedes_id`.
- **Payout** is a first-class record for reimbursements and vendor payments: `payout` (document_type, document_id, amount_paise, paid_on, org_account_id, payment_reference, paid_by, status pending/confirmed/failed). Each payout is its own posting source. A reimbursement is `paid` when confirmed payouts cover the approved amount.
- **Vendor invoice**: `vendor_invoice` header (supplier identity snapshot, supplier_gstin, recipient_gstin, invoice_number, invoice_date, place_of_supply, reverse_charge, gross_total_paise) and `vendor_invoice_line` (taxable_value_paise, hsn_sac, gst_rate_bp, cgst, sgst_utgst, igst, cess, recoverable_tax_paise, account head and dimensions). For gross G and recoverable tax T, recognise expense or capital asset G − T and a recoverable-tax asset T. Payments before the invoice are supplier advances until allocated.
- **Invoice allocation**: `vendor_invoice_allocation` (invoice_id, `kind` (payment, supplier_advance, withholding), payout_id or withholding_id, amount_paise). Outstanding = G − allocations. An amount cannot be allocated to two invoices. Status recomputation reads confirmed allocations, replacing the current comparison of approved transactions against line totals.
- **Withholding (TDS)**: `withholding` (vendor party, invoice or payment allocation, `provision` (section 393 table item), taxable_base_paise, rate_bp, amount_paise, trigger_on = earlier of credit or payment, deducted_on, lower_or_nil_basis, payee tax snapshot). A deduction made at a pre-invoice payment is not deducted again at invoice. `tax_remittance` (paid_on, account_movement_id, challan identifiers, tax year and quarter, allocations to withholdings). Remittance clears the liability and is not vendor expense.
- **Cash vouchers**: `voucher` table (fiscal_year_id, `number` FY sequence, line_item_id, attachment_id, generated_at). The number is allocated in the approval transaction and reused on PDF retry.

### 3.7 Budgets (deferred)

`budget` (fiscal_year_id, concrete FK per scope, `measure` = recognised_cost only in the first version, revision, status, approved_by) and `budget_line` (account_head_id, amount_paise). Unique approved revision per scope, fiscal year and measure. Cash-basis budgets wait for dimensioned movement allocations. Fund and programme views are alternative cuts, never summed.

### 3.8 Posting layer

```
posting
  id, source_type, source_id, source_revision, action,
  idempotency_key UNIQUE (source_type, source_id, source_revision, action),
  created_by, created_at

ledger_entry                       -- recognition
  id, posting_id, source_line_id,
  economic_on, posted_on, fiscal_period_id,
  effect (increase | decrease), amount_paise,
  account_head_id, fund_id, programme_id?, project_id?, event_id?, cost_centre_id?,
  party_id?, medium (monetary | in_kind),
  fcra_activity?, fcra_expense_class? (programme | asset | administration),
  reverses_entry_id?

account_movement                   -- cash book
  id, posting_id, org_account_id,
  occurred_on, posted_on, bank_value_on?, fiscal_period_id,
  direction (in | out), amount_paise,
  kind (receipt | payout | transfer | refund | fee | tax_remittance | opening),
  payment_reference?, transfer_id?, reverses_movement_id?

movement_allocation
  account_movement_id, document_type, document_id, amount_paise
```

Posting triggers (each row is one source action with its own source ID):

| Source action | Recognition | Movement |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution received (gateway) | income gross; fee and fee tax as expense | in (gross) to gateway clearing; fee out |
| Gateway settlement to bank | none | transfer out of clearing, in to bank |
| Contribution received (bank, UPI, cheque, cash) | income gross | in to that account |
| Other income received | income | in |
| Issued invoice sent | fee income (gross) | none |
| Issued invoice receipt confirmed | TDS receivable asset for the deducted part | in (net cash) |
| Donor refund | income decrease | refund out |
| In-kind contribution | in-kind income plus consumption expense or asset increase | none |
| Reimbursement approved | expense per line | none |
| Payout confirmed (reimbursement or vendor) | none | payout out |
| Settlement round approved | expense per settlement line | none |
| Vendor invoice accepted | expense or asset G − T; recoverable-tax asset T | none |
| Withholding deducted | none (liability on the withholding row) | none |
| Tax remittance paid | none | tax_remittance out |
| Inter-account transfer | none | linked out and in sharing `transfer_id` |
| Correction document approved | reversal plus replacement | only with evidence of a movement |
| Opening balance (cutover) | none | opening in, CA-confirmed |

Rules:

1. Append-only. No update or delete path in the product. Corrections append a reversal and a replacement from a superseding document. A classification correction never creates a movement.
2. The server mutator serialises posting against document changes, allocation limits and period closure, rechecks permissions and document state, enforces the idempotency key, and returns rejections through `handleMutationResult()`. Optimistic client state never allocates receipt or voucher numbers.
3. Posted documents and lines are frozen even in open periods. `requests.edit_all_statuses` and `resetToPending` apply only to unposted revisions.
4. Late documents keep their economic dates. Posting into a closed year is refused until the year is reopened (`finance.reopen_year`, with a reason, audited).
5. `medium = in_kind` entries are excluded from cash views and labelled separately in the CA handoff; they are included in programme cost and the audit pack.
6. Adjustments need `finance.adjust` and a reason stored on the adjustment document.
7. Authenticated writes keep the existing audit boundaries: Zero commands through the central audited mutation boundary in `apps/web/src/routes/api/zero/mutate.ts`, direct server commands through `runSessionAuditedAction`. Finance actions extend the action summaries and safe-field allowlists in `apps/web/src/lib/audit.ts`; metadata carries only action IDs, entity IDs, changed field names and counts. Downstream jobs are enqueued after commit.

### 3.9 Imports and reconciliation

| Table | Key columns |
|---|---|
| `import_batch` | `source` (razorpay_api, give_do_csv, give_do_payout, bank_csv, upi_csv), org_account_id, uploaded_by, file attachment, file_hash, parser_version, mapping (jsonb), row counts, status |
| `import_row` | batch_id, row_number, raw (jsonb, server only, short retention), raw_hash, external_identity_id, outcome (imported, linked_existing, conflict, skipped, error) |
| `external_identity` | provider, provider_account, entity_type (payment, order, refund, settlement, statement_line), external_id, exactly one target: contribution_id, account_movement_id or statement_line_id. UNIQUE on the four identity columns. Survives raw payload expiry |
| `statement_line` | org_account_id, value_on, amount_paise, direction, narration, reference, reconciliation_status |
| `statement_allocation` | statement_line_id, account_movement_id, amount_paise |

Rules:

- `external_identity` is the only identity store; contributions and import rows reference it. Strong external identity is the only automatic dedupe. Amount, date and contact fingerprints only propose candidate matches for review.
- Identical re-uploads link to the earlier result. A changed payload under the same identity becomes a conflict. Retrying a batch processes unresolved rows only. Posted contributions change through corrections, never import overwrite.
- Bank and UPI files create statement lines, which reconcile by allocation against movements. Gateway settlements, transfers and refunds never create donations. A partially allocated line stays visibly unreconciled.
- Shipped matching (layer 3d, before movements exist): a statement line is matched whole to what explains it, held on the line (`matched_settlement_id`, `matched_contribution_id`, each unique, or a kind with no target: give.do payout, transfer). Only credits are matched. Exact evidence matches automatically; everything else is a suggestion a reviewer accepts or turns down. In order: (1) a processed give.do live settlement or Razorpay settlement of the server's mode (live or test) whose UTR is a whole token of the line's reference or narration and whose net amount equals the line's matches; its UTR with another amount is shown as related information without a Match, and the same amount settled within ±3 days is suggested; (2) a contribution recorded as deposited into this account by UPI, NEFT, cheque or cash, received, with the same amount, received within ±3 days and the same reference when both have one (compared by whole tokens of at least four characters with a digit, not all zeros; punctuation and a cheque number's leading zeros ignored) matches; when either has no reference it is suggested, and different references never pair; (3) a narration naming GiveIndia or give.do (`GIVEINDIA_NARRATION_PATTERNS`) is suggested as a give.do payout. Each settlement and contribution goes to one line, and a record a reviewer turned down or unmatched is never matched or suggested for that line again. A line never creates a donation: "Record as donation" is a manual contribution (source `manual`). Phase 3 turns these matches into `statement_allocation` rows against account movements; until then a line that only part of a record explains stays unmatched.
- Razorpay import pulls payments, refunds and settlements separately, preserving gross, fee, tax and settlement allocation.

**Import hashes and the key.** Import row hashes, payload hashes and fingerprint IDs (`private.import_row.raw_hash`, `private.external_identity.payload_hash`, and `external_id` for files without an external ID column) are HMAC-SHA256 under a key derived from `DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, or, outside production while that key is unset, from `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`. The `h1:` prefix names the scheme only; there is no key ID. Setting `DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY` where the fallback was used, or replacing the production key, silently changes every new hash: a re-upload of a file without an external ID column is no longer recognised as already imported (its fingerprint IDs differ, so its rows would import again unless the duplicate check flags them), and a re-upload of rows with external IDs shows each one as a conflict, "changed since imported", because the stored payload hash no longer matches. Treat the key as fixed once imports exist; changing it needs a re-hash of the stored values, which is not built.

## 4. Data protection

Follows the DPDP compliance guide (Documents, Proud Indian private, 8 October 2026) and its retention schedule, with retention held per record.

- Every retained finance record, attachment, receipt, filing row and import payload carries `retention_policy_version`, `retain_until`, `retention_basis` and `legal_hold`. Financial evidence keeps ten years from the end of the tax year; import payloads keep months; money-related audit rows keep ten years, others thirteen months.
- Expiry is handled by a privileged retention process, not ordinary product mutations. It preserves referential integrity for append-only books and records each disposal separately.
- An erasure request removes CRM and communication data immediately where permitted. Identifying evidence under statutory retention or legal hold stays, under restricted access, until the obligation expires; the request log records what was kept and why.
- Encrypted at rest and excluded from Zero: `party_private.id_number`, `party_bank_account.account_number`, `org_account` bank details, payee account numbers on reimbursements and legacy advance payments, filing-row donor IDs. Snapshots and attachments containing the same data are server-only and served through guarded URLs.
- `collection_event` records the processing basis and notice version per collection. A donor who paid on the Razorpay page saw that page's notice; a donor imported from a give.do CSV did not see ours, and the record says so. Receipt processing (section 7(a)) and statutory disclosure (section 7(d)) do not authorise fundraising communication; that needs its own consent purpose.
- Finance parties survive login deletion.
- Receipt and certificate PDFs and emails run after commit through `enqueue()` with `withFireAndForgetLog()` and a stable key of document ID plus revision, so a retry reuses the same receipt number.

## 8. Remaining open items

1. Fiscal years: the `ensure-fiscal-years` job runs on the 1st of every month and keeps the current and next April-to-March year present with their periods, so a year exists from the April a year before it starts. Closed.
2. Engagement receipts are donations, not fee income. The audited statements for 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26 (GVNKA & Associates) show all income under "Donations received" with no service or fee line, and the accounting policy recognises donations on receipt. So Goodera and corporate engagement money enters as `contribution` with `donation_type = csr` (direct corporate) or `other` (via Goodera) and a sponsor on the engagement project. `issued_invoice` is kept only as the document the sponsor asks for before paying; it does not recognise income, and the receivables rows in section 3.4 and the posting table are downgraded to "acknowledgement sent, no posting until the contribution is received". The section 12AB incidental-business question therefore does not arise unless the CA changes the treatment.
3. GST: not needed as of now (founder, 14 October 2026). Issued acknowledgements carry no GST; vendor invoices still record the GST vendors charge us. Revisit only if the trust starts making taxable supplies.
